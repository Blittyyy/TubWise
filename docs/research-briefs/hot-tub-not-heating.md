# TubWise Article Research Brief

## Working title

Hot Tub Not Heating: What to Check First

## Target search query

hot tub not heating

## Search intent

The reader owns a hot tub that is not reaching the set temperature and wants to identify safe checks before calling a technician.

## Reader situation

- The spa appears to have some function (or the reader is unsure), but water is not heating as expected.
- The reader wants a safe first-pass checklist that does not require opening equipment or performing electrical diagnosis.
- The reader may have recently drained/refilled, cleaned filters, or noticed a tripped breaker/GFCI or panel message.

## Article scope

Safe first checks only:

1. Confirm whether the entire spa has power.
2. Check the displayed set temperature and, where the owner's manual documents one, a filtration/lock mode setting. **VERIFIED, narrow scope only:** Hot Spring's own troubleshooting chart frames this as "spa lock," not a general "operating mode" (see Source A). A separate, older Jacuzzi Premium ProTech LED owner's manual (Source C, 2002, J-310/J-320/J-330/J-340 models only) instructs checking whether the spa is in "Economy Filtration Mode" as a step in its "No Heat" procedure, because in that mode "water temperature is not thermostatically controlled." This is model-specific and must not be presented as a universal "check your operating mode" step — phrase it as "check your model's control panel or manual for an economy/eco filtration setting, spa lock, or similar mode."
3. Look for an error code or warning light / indicator.
4. Confirm the water level is adequate.
5. Check whether water is circulating normally (observable jet/flow behavior; not internal pump diagnosis).
6. Inspect and clean the filter according to the manufacturer’s directions.
7. Consider an air lock if the problem started after draining or refilling.
8. Check whether the breaker or GFCI has tripped once.
9. Stop if the breaker/GFCI trips again.
10. Contact a technician if normal flow does not return or the spa still will not heat.

Important distinctions to keep in the article framing (scope note):

- A spa with no power is different from a spa that has power but no heat. **Verified — Hot Spring guidance directly separates “spa is inoperative” from “spa won’t heat while jets and lights operate.”**
- Slow heating is different from no heating. **VERIFIED, narrow scope only.** A legacy Jacuzzi Premium ProTech LED owner's manual (Source C, 2002, J-310/J-320/J-330/J-340 models, 240V operation) states: "Smaller spas heat at a rate of approximately 8 to 10 degrees per hour (based on 240V operation); large spas heat at about 4 to 6 degrees per hour." This is a single legacy model line's documented figure, not a universal rate, and the same document gives no comparable 120V figure. Use only as an illustrative example ("heat-up can normally take hours, per manufacturer documentation") — do not present as a modern or cross-brand standard.
- Plug-and-play 120V spas universally heat more slowly or lose temperature during use. **REMOVE FROM ARTICLE.** No source in this brief makes this general claim. The only 120V-related manufacturer detail found (Source C, 2002 J-310 convertible model, 15A/30A configuration) states only that "the heater will not operate at the same time as the high-speed jets pump" on that specific legacy model/configuration — a wiring-jumper interlock detail, not a "heats slower" claim, and too old/narrow to generalize. Do not include any 120V-heats-slower statement in the article.
- Error-code meanings and reset procedures vary by brand and control system. **Verified in principle — Hot Spring and Jacuzzi use different indicator names, colors, and error codes even between their own model lines (see Manufacturer-specific differences). Do not invent code tables.**
- Do not diagnose a failed heating element from symptoms alone. **Editorial safety rule; no source in this brief claims symptom-only heater-element diagnosis is valid. Reinforced by Source C, which requires an authorized dealer to repair sensor faults (SN1/SN2) rather than homeowner diagnosis.**

## Out of scope

- Opening a control box or equipment bay for inspection of wiring or internal components
- Touching wiring or energized equipment
- Multimeter testing
- Bypassing a pressure switch, flow switch, sensor, GFCI, or high-limit device
- Repeatedly resetting a breaker/GFCI after it trips again
- Heater-element replacement or heater wiring diagnosis
- Brand-agnostic “this is always your failed heater” conclusions
- Chemical dosing or water-balance procedures beyond noting circulation/filtration relevance
- DIY electrical panel work beyond a single visible GFCI/breaker reset when the unit is otherwise safe and the manufacturer allows it

## Brand, model, or control system

- **Not brand-exclusive.** Article is a safe first-check overview.
- Include manufacturer-specific examples from Hot Spring and Jacuzzi only where sourced.
- Require readers to use their model’s owner’s manual for indicator meanings, filter procedures, water-level marks, and error codes.
- Do not assume one brand’s indicator behavior applies to another.

## Direct answer

Write a one- to three-sentence answer supported by sources.

**Draft (source-bound):** Before assuming the heater has failed, manufacturer troubleshooting for major spa brands points to simpler issues first: loss of power or a tripped GFCI, reduced flow from low water or a clogged filter, a high-limit or flow/pressure safety condition, or an air lock after filling. If the GFCI will not reset, trips again, or a high-limit/heater trip condition returns after basic flow and filter checks, stop and arrange qualified service.

## Safe homeowner checks

List checks that can be performed without opening energized equipment or bypassing safety systems.

1. **Confirm spa has power / panel indicators respond**  
   - Hot Spring distinguishes inoperative spa (power/GFCI/high-limit/lock) from “won’t heat while jets and lights operate.”  
   - Source: Hot Spring troubleshooting guide. Confidence: High.

2. **Check set temperature display**  
   - **VERIFIED for Hot Spring Highlife Collection only.** Highlife OM: “The TEAL indicator will light when the spa water temperature is within +/- 2°F (1°C) of the set temperature, and will turn off when the temperature is outside of that range.” Note: a separate, undated Hot Spring troubleshooting blog page (Source A) refers to a “green logo ‘Ready’” indicator for its general chart — indicator color is not consistent across Hot Spring’s own materials/model generations, so do not assert one universal color.  
   - Source: Highlife OM (Source B), effective 12/22, covering 2023 Highlife Collection models (Grandee, Envoy, Vanguard, Aria, Sovereign, Prodigy, Jetsetter LX, Jetsetter). Confidence: High for that model family; do not generalize the indicator color or the ±2°F range to other brands or older Hot Spring lines without their own manual.

3. **Check for a spa lock or documented economy/filtration mode**  
   - Hot Spring: “Spa lock is activated” is listed as a cause of an inoperative spa; solution “Deactivate the spa lock.” Source: Hot Spring troubleshooting guide (Source A). Confidence: High for Hot Spring.  
   - Separately, a legacy Jacuzzi Premium ProTech LED manual (Source C, 2002, J-310/J-320/J-330/J-340 only) lists checking for “Economy Filtration Mode” as a step in its own “No Heat” procedure, because that mode does not thermostatically hold temperature. Confidence: High for that specific legacy model line only — **do not present “check operating mode” as generic advice across brands.**

4. **Look for error codes or warning/diagnostic lights**  
   - Hot Spring: blinking Power indicator = high-limit diagnostic.  
   - Jacuzzi: FL1/FL2 flow errors deactivate heater.  
   - Sources: Highlife OM; Jacuzzi PDF 562. Confidence: High within those systems. Emphasize model-specific manuals.

5. **Confirm adequate water level**  
   - **VERIFIED with exact quotes, both model-specific — do not merge into one universal number.**  
   - Jacuzzi Premium ProTech LED manual (Source C, 2002, J-310/J-320/J-330/J-340 only): fill instructions state “Continue filling spa until the water level is 1 inch below the lowest pillow and above all jets.” FL1/FL2 correction step: “Check for proper water level. Water should be 1” below lowest pillow.”  
   - Jacuzzi consumer pump-troubleshooting page (Source E, jacuzzi.com, undated): “The waterline should sit at least 1–2 inches above the highest jet, typically at mid-skimmer level.” This is a different figure from a different Jacuzzi document/model scope — present both only as brand examples, not as one number.  
   - Hot Spring: low water listed among causes of weak/surging jets (flow symptom adjacent to heat/flow issues). Source: Hot Spring troubleshooting guide (Source A).  
   - Confidence: High that low water is a manufacturer-recognized check across both brands; exact fill line is always model-specific — instruct readers to use their own manual’s mark.

6. **Observe whether water is circulating normally**  
   - Frame as external observation (jets weak/surging, obvious lack of circulation), not pump teardown.  
   - Hot Spring: weak/surging jets → low water or clogged filters.  
   - Source: Hot Spring troubleshooting guide. Confidence: High for that symptom pair.

7. **Inspect/clean filters per manufacturer directions**  
   - Repeatedly listed by Hot Spring for heat/high-limit/pressure-switch/flow issues.  
   - Jacuzzi: clogged/excessively dirty circulation filter as FL/heat-related cause.  
   - Sources: Hot Spring guide; Jacuzzi PDF 562/681. Confidence: High. Cleaning method must defer to the model manual.

8. **Consider air lock after drain/refill**  
   - Hot Spring: air lock after filling as cause when spa won’t heat while jets/lights operate; hose-down-filter-compartment fill guidance in their chart.  
   - Jacuzzi: air lock at circulation pump intake; purge guidance in model manuals.  
   - Sources: Hot Spring guide; Jacuzzi PDF 562. Confidence: High for “consider after refill”; Medium that one purge method fits all brands — use model instructions only.

9. **Check breaker/GFCI tripped status once**  
   - **Required framing for the article: “Reset once only if permitted by the owner’s manual. If it will not reset or trips again, stop.”**  
   - Hot Spring, exact quote: “Reset the GFCI”; “Call for spa service if the GFCI won’t reset.”  
   - Jacuzzi, exact quote: “Reset once by flipping the breaker fully off, then on. If it immediately trips again, stop—this signals an electrical fault requiring professional attention.”  
   - Sources: Hot Spring guide (Source A); Jacuzzi pump page (Source E). Confidence: High for single check/reset framing.

## Technician-only steps

List anything that should not be presented as a DIY procedure.

- Diagnosis or repair when GFCI will not reset (Hot Spring: call for spa service / electrician as applicable).
- Service when high-limit condition continues or trips frequently (Hot Spring).
- Service when heater or circulation-pump protective trips are frequent (Hot Spring).
- Flow-switch testing / internal switch diagnosis (Jacuzzi service manuals reference technician testing sections — do not reproduce DIY electrical tests).
- Any work requiring opening the control box or equipment compartment with exposure to wiring.
- Multimeter testing, heater-element testing/replacement, bypassing pressure/flow/high-limit/GFCI devices.
- Electrical fault diagnosis after repeated breaker trips (Jacuzzi: professional attention).
- Pump or heater mechanical/electrical repair by unqualified persons (editorial + Jacuzzi professional-service framing).

## Step-by-step troubleshooting order

Proposed order for the article (aligned to requested safe checks; each step must cite or defer to the owner’s manual):

1. Confirm whether the spa has power (panel on? lights/jets respond?).
2. Note set temperature and any lock or documented economy/filtration-mode state shown on the panel. **Resolved:** frame only as “check your model’s manual for a spa lock or economy/eco mode” — verified as Hot Spring “spa lock” (Source A) and, separately, a legacy Jacuzzi “Economy Filtration Mode” (Source C, 2002, J-310/J-320/J-330/J-340 only). Do not describe this as a universal “operating mode” check.
3. Record any error code or diagnostic indicator (e.g., Hot Spring blinking Power; Jacuzzi FL1/FL2/SN1/SN2 codes).
4. Check water level against the model manual’s mark (verified example figures exist for specific Jacuzzi documents only — see Manufacturer-specific differences; do not quote one number as universal).
5. Observe circulation/jet behavior for obvious low flow.
6. Clean/inspect filters using manufacturer directions.
7. If recently drained/refilled, follow the manufacturer’s air-lock / refill priming instructions only.
8. If the spa is dead, check whether GFCI/breaker has tripped. **Reset once only if permitted by the owner’s manual. If it will not reset or trips again, stop.**
9. If it will not reset or trips again after one attempt, stop and arrange qualified service.
10. If power and apparent flow look normal but heat still fails, or protective trips return → technician.

## Stop conditions

- **Reset once only if permitted by the owner’s manual. If it will not reset or trips again, stop.** (Article-wide required phrasing for all GFCI/breaker guidance; consistent with Hot Spring’s “Call for spa service if the GFCI won’t reset” and Jacuzzi’s “Reset once... If it immediately trips again, stop—this signals an electrical fault requiring professional attention.”)
- GFCI/breaker will not reset (Hot Spring: call service/electrician as applicable).
- GFCI/breaker trips again after one reset (Jacuzzi: stop; electrical fault requiring professional attention).
- High-limit / heater protective condition returns or trips frequently (Hot Spring: call for spa service).
- Circulation pump protective trips are frequent (Hot Spring).
- Error/flow condition persists after manufacturer filter/level/air-lock checks (Jacuzzi: contact authorized dealer).
- Any need to open the control box, test wiring, bypass safeties, or replace heater components.

## When to call a technician

- GFCI won’t reset or immediately re-trips.
- High-limit or heater trip conditions continue after basic filter/flow checks.
- No normal circulation returns after manufacturer-directed filter/level/air-lock steps.
- Spa still will not heat after the safe checklist.
- Any suspected electrical fault, burning smell, scorched components, or wiring issues. **Resolved: no source in this brief attributes this phrase to a manufacturer. Retain only as a general, unattributed safety editorial stop condition — do not cite a source for it.**
