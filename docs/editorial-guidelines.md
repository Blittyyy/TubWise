# TubWise Editorial Guidelines

## Purpose

TubWise publishes clear, accurate, practical guidance for hot tub owners.

## Core standards

- Never invent facts, statistics, quotes, credentials, product testing, or personal experience.
- Never imply that TubWise inspected or tested something unless that actually happened.
- Every technical claim must be supported by a reliable source.
- Prefer manufacturer manuals, control-system documentation, government safety guidance, and established industry sources.
- State clearly when advice varies by brand, model, control system, or local code.
- Do not present a likely cause as a confirmed diagnosis.
- Separate safe homeowner checks from technician-only work.
- Never instruct readers to open or touch energized electrical equipment.
- Never tell readers to repeatedly reset a tripping breaker.
- Recommend qualified service when electrical, structural, gas, persistent leak, or safety issues are involved.
- Do not publish placeholder citations or unverifiable sources.
- Update articles when manufacturer guidance changes.

## Writing style

- Give the direct answer near the top.
- Use short paragraphs.
- Use descriptive H2 and H3 headings.
- Write in a calm, professional, plain-English voice.
- Be specific rather than generic.
- Explain uncertainty honestly.
- Avoid filler and exaggerated language.
- Avoid unnecessary jargon.
- Define technical terms when first used.

## Phrases to avoid

- Let’s dive in
- You’re not alone
- In today’s fast-paced world
- Whether you are a beginner or an expert
- This comprehensive guide
- Everything you need to know
- Game changer
- Unlock
- Revolutionize
- Seamless
- Hassle-free
- Simply
- Obviously
- Always
- Never, unless it is a true safety rule

## Troubleshooting article structure

1. Direct answer
2. Safety note
3. Most likely causes
4. Safe checks in order
5. What each result means
6. When to stop
7. When to call a technician
8. Related guides
9. Sources

## Error-code article rules

- Identify the manufacturer, brand, model, or control system.
- Do not assume the same code means the same thing across all systems.
- Quote or closely paraphrase the official manual.
- Explain only safe homeowner checks.
- Clearly separate manufacturer-specific guidance from general background.
- Include the exact source manual or support page.

## Water-care article rules

- Do not provide chemical dosing unless the formula and units are verified.
- State that product concentrations vary.
- Encourage readers to follow the product label and manufacturer instructions.
- Avoid medical claims.
- Distinguish sanitation, pH, alkalinity, hardness, and filtration.

## Product and buying-guide rules

- Do not claim firsthand testing unless it occurred.
- State the basis for recommendations.
- Disclose affiliate relationships.
- Avoid ranking products solely from retailer descriptions.
- Prefer compatibility, specifications, warranty, and documented features.

## Citations

- Keep a source list for every article.
- Use primary sources whenever possible.
- Support safety claims with authoritative references.
- Do not cite low-quality scraped sites when better sources exist.

## Final review checklist

- Is every factual claim supported?
- Is the brand or model scope clear?
- Are homeowner-safe and technician-only steps separated?
- Are uncertainty and limitations stated?
- Are there any invented facts or experiences?
- Are all links and sources valid?
- Is the direct answer near the top?
- Does the article avoid filler?
- Are there clear stop conditions?
- Is the article useful without affiliate links?
