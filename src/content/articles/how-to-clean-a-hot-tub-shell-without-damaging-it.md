## Direct answer

Clean the acrylic shell with products your manufacturer lists as safe for that surface. Hot Spring Hot Spot and Highlife manuals state that the only approved shell cleaners are plain water, Hot Spring FreshWater Spa Shine, or Soft Scrub, and they warn that other household cleaners, including products such as Windex and Simple Green, can damage the shell and that such damage is not covered by warranty (Hot Spring Hot Spot Collection owner's manual; Hot Spring Highlife owner's manual). Jacuzzi describes an acrylic shell that needs an occasional damp cloth and gentle soap, with abrasive cleaners avoided and soap suds wiped away before refilling (Jacuzzi).

## Safety note

Keep cleaners out of reach of children (Hot Spring Hot Spot Collection owner's manual). Work on an empty spa when you are doing a full wipe-down during a drain, and keep liquids away from the control pack and any open electrical areas. Rinse cleaning agents completely so residue does not remain when you refill (Hot Spring; Jacuzzi). Do not invent a universal cleaner list that merges Hot Spring and Jacuzzi approvals.

## Brand rules are not interchangeable

Hot Spring's approved list is narrow: plain water, FreshWater Spa Shine, or Soft Scrub only (Hot Spring Hot Spot Collection owner's manual). Alcohol and other household cleaners are not recommended, and abrasive or solvent cleaners are called out as shell hazards, with Simple Green, Windex, and Spa Mitt named specifically (Hot Spring Hot Spot Collection owner's manual). Hot Spot quick-start guidance also warns that most household chemicals are harmful, especially Windex or citrus products (Hot Spring Hot Spot Collection quick start guide).

Jacuzzi's exterior-care page allows a damp cloth and gentle soap on the acrylic shell and offers Jacuzzi Spa All-Purpose Cleaner for shell and skirt, with dealer contact for more aggressive cleaning needs (Jacuzzi). That is not permission to use Hot Spring's Soft Scrub path on a Jacuzzi, or Jacuzzi all-purpose cleaner on a Hot Spring, unless your own manual agrees.

## When to clean the shell

Hot Spring's maintenance schedule places a full interior wipe-down after the spa is drained during seasonal water changes (Hot Spring). Pair shell cleaning with [How Often Should You Drain and Refill a Hot Tub?](/maintenance/how-often-should-you-drain-and-refill-a-hot-tub). Between drains, Hot Spring notes that stains and dirt generally do not adhere and that a soft rag removes most dirt (Hot Spring Hot Spot Collection owner's manual). Jacuzzi likewise describes only occasional cleaning for the nonporous acrylic shell (Jacuzzi). For a ring of oils and dirt at the waterline specifically, see [Why Is There a Ring Around My Hot Tub Waterline?](/water-care/why-is-there-a-ring-around-my-hot-tub-waterline).

## Safe cleaning sequence

1. **Confirm your brand's approved cleaners** in the owner's manual before buying anything new.
2. **Drain the spa** if you are doing a full interior clean, following your manual's power and drain steps.
3. **Wipe the shell** with a soft cloth. Hot Spring allows a nylon scrubber for remaining dirt in its maintenance page language (Hot Spring). Jacuzzi emphasizes avoiding abrasives (Jacuzzi).
4. **Use only an approved cleaner** for your brand. Apply sparingly.
5. **Rinse thoroughly with fresh water** and remove all suds before refill (Hot Spring; Jacuzzi).
6. **Clean pillows** separately with mild soap and water when Hot Spring's seasonal guidance applies (Hot Spring).
7. **Refill and restart** only after the shell is free of cleaner residue.

For debris on the water surface between drains, a spa vacuum can help without scrubbing the acrylic; see [How to Choose a Hot Tub Vacuum](/buying-guides/how-to-choose-a-hot-tub-vacuum).

## Mineral stains and scale

Hot Spring warns that iron and copper in the water can stain the shell if left unchecked and directs owners to ask a dealer about a stain and scale inhibitor, recommending FreshWater Stain & Scale Defense in that manual context (Hot Spring Hot Spot Collection owner's manual). Stain prevention is water care, not a reason to scrub with banned household products. For rust-colored shell marks, see [Why Does My Hot Tub Have Rust-Colored Stains?](/water-care/why-does-my-hot-tub-have-rust-colored-stains). For chalky calcium deposits, see [Why Is There Scale on My Hot Tub Shell?](/water-care/why-is-there-scale-on-my-hot-tub-shell).

## What damages acrylic shells

Avoid on Hot Spring shells unless your manual explicitly allows them: alcohol, Windex, Simple Green, Spa Mitt, abrasive cleaners, and solvent cleaners (Hot Spring Hot Spot Collection owner's manual). Avoid abrasive cleaners on Jacuzzi acrylic, and do not leave soap suds in the shell before refill (Jacuzzi). Do not import pool-deck or glass cleaners from general household use.

Cover cleaning is a separate job with its own soap rules; see [How to Clean a Hot Tub Cover](/maintenance/how-to-clean-a-hot-tub-cover).

## When to stop and call a dealer

- Stains remain after approved cleaners and a full rinse
- The shell is scratched, cracked, or blistered
- You need a cleaner stronger than your manual allows
- Discoloration returns quickly and may track to metal content in fill water
- Warranty language in your manual ties shell damage to unapproved chemicals
