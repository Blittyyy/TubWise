## Direct answer

Overnight water loss can be evaporation through an open or poorly sealing cover, or it can be a plumbing leak. Jacuzzi describes both natural evaporation and pump or heater leaks as reasons water levels change (Jacuzzi). Jacuzzi also tells owners to latch the cover when the spa is not in use to limit heat loss and evaporation, especially overnight (Jacuzzi). Start with the cover and a dry-pad check before assuming a fitting failed.

## Safety note

If you suspect a leak rather than evaporation, Jacuzzi advises turning off the power supply (Jacuzzi). Do not open the control box. Do not chase leaks into foam or under the shell as a DIY project. Water near electrical equipment is a technician stop condition.

## Clues that point toward evaporation

Jacuzzi states that a quality cover reduces water evaporation, and that damaged or waterlogged covers lose effectiveness (Jacuzzi). Jacuzzi's energy guidance says to latch the cover when the spa is idle to prevent heat loss and evaporation overnight and in windy or cold weather (Jacuzzi). Hot Spot Collection manuals likewise note that spa water evaporates and that keeping the cover in place helps minimize that loss (Hot Spring Hot Spot Collection guide).

Evaporation-leaning clues:

- No wet spots on the pad, cabinet base, or plumbing
- Cover left open, propped, or obviously out of square
- Cover feels heavy, sagging, or no longer seals at the skirt or hinge
- Loss is gradual with the spa idle and jets off

Jacuzzi's swim spa maintenance guide states that a cover that no longer seals properly can accelerate evaporation, potentially about an inch of water weekly in that guidance (Jacuzzi). That figure is Jacuzzi swim spa maintenance language, not a universal overnight rate for every residential spa.

If you need a better-fitting cover later, see [How to Choose a Hot Tub Cover](/buying-guides/how-to-choose-a-hot-tub-cover). Choosing a cover does not by itself prove last night's drop was evaporation.

## Clues that point toward a leak

Jacuzzi tells owners to look for wet spots or pooling around the spa, particularly near plumbing connections, and to power off if a leak is suspected (Jacuzzi). Jacuzzi also names pump or heater leaks among causes of water-level problems (Jacuzzi).

Leak-leaning clues:

- Fresh damp ground, cabinet stains, or active drips
- Water under the spa or inside the equipment bay
- Level keeps dropping with a closed, well-seated cover and no spa use
- Loss that speeds up when jets run (see the jets-on guide below)

For under-spa wetness, continue with [Hot Tub Leaking From Underneath: What to Check](/troubleshooting/hot-tub-leaking-from-underneath). For a broader leak overview, see [Why Is My Hot Tub Leaking?](/troubleshooting/why-is-my-hot-tub-leaking).

## How to compare the two without inventing a test kit

Manufacturers do not require a branded "bucket test" product for this decision. A practical homeowner comparison is:

1. **Mark the waterline** before bed with the cover latched.
2. **Check the mark in the morning** before anyone uses the spa.
3. **Inspect for moisture** on the pad and at cabinet plumbing with power off if the level fell.
4. **Repeat once with deliberate cover care** (fully seated and strapped) if the first night's cover fit was questionable.

If the level falls and the area stays dry, focus on cover seal and evaporation. If the level falls and you find wet plumbing or under-spa water, treat it as a leak path and stop before electrical parts.

## Safe checks the homeowner can do

1. **Latch and seat the cover** whenever the spa is idle (Jacuzzi).
2. **Inspect the cover** for sagging, cracks, heavy waterlogged feel, or gaps at the skirt (Jacuzzi).
3. **Confirm the fill line** after topping off. Jacuzzi describes keeping water just above the skimmer intake as part of stable operation (Jacuzzi).
4. **Power off and look for wet spots** if the overnight drop continues with a closed cover.
5. **Call service** if you cannot separate evaporation from a leak, or if water is near electrical equipment.

## When to stop and call a technician

- Overnight loss continues with a sound cover seal and a dry-looking pad, but you still cannot explain the drop
- You find water in the equipment bay or under the spa
- Any next step would mean cutting foam, pressure-testing lines, or replacing seals
- Water is near the control pack or heater electrical connections
