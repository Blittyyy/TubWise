## Direct answer

If heat seems to appear only while jets or filtration are running, start with how your spa couples the heater to pump operation and operating mode. Hot Spot documents heater output that depends on pump speed and electrical configuration, including cases where a 115-volt heater runs only with the pump on low speed (Hot Spot). Jacuzzi describes Economy mode as heating during filter cycles rather than holding set temperature continuously (Jacuzzi). Those explanations are brand- and mode-scoped. They are not a universal rule that every spa should heat only with jets on.

## Safety note

Do not open the control box or change wiring to force the heater and jets to run together. Do not bypass flow switches, pressure switches, high-limit devices, or GFCIs. Do not run jets continuously as a heating workaround. If the spa overheats, trips breakers repeatedly, smells like burning, or behaves unpredictably, shut off power and contact a qualified spa technician.

## Separate this symptom from no heat

**Heats only when jets or filtration run:** Water warms during jet or filter operation, then stalls or cools when those pumps are off.

**Will not heat even with jets and lights working:** Hot Spring documents that pattern as a different problem and lists an open pressure switch, circulation-pump thermal cut-off, or air lock among the causes (Hot Spring). See [Hot Tub Has Power but No Heat](/troubleshooting/hot-tub-has-power-but-no-heat).

**Weak jets plus poor heat:** Flow may be the shared issue. See [Why Are My Hot Tub Jets Weak?](/troubleshooting/why-are-my-hot-tub-jets-weak) and [Can a Dirty Filter Stop a Hot Tub From Heating?](/troubleshooting/can-a-dirty-filter-stop-a-hot-tub-from-heating).

## Manufacturer-scoped reasons heat can track pump activity

### Pump speed and electrical configuration (Hot Spot)

On certain Hot Spot 115-volt setups, the heater provides about 1000 watts only when the pump operates on low speed and the thermostat calls for heat. In that same 115-volt configuration, the heater and jet pump cannot operate at the same time, so the heater turns off while the jet pump runs (Hot Spot). Owners sometimes describe low-speed pump activity as "jets on low." Confirm the exact electrical configuration and panel behavior in the Hot Spot manual for your model. Do not attempt a 230-volt conversion yourself.

### Economy or filter-cycle heating (Jacuzzi)

Jacuzzi's energy guide describes Standard mode as maintaining set temperature continuously and Economy mode as heating only during filter cycles, with temperature allowed to fluctuate between sessions (Jacuzzi). A legacy Jacuzzi ProTech manual for specific older models also documents an economy filtration mode that does not control temperature continuously (Jacuzzi ProTech). If your spa is in Economy or a similar label, heat may appear mainly when filtration runs. Check your own manual before changing modes.

### Controllers that require flow before heating (Gecko example)

Gecko in.xe quick-reference material describes a regulation cycle that first generates water flow through the heater housing, then turns the heater on to reach and maintain set point after verifying pump activation and temperature reading (Gecko Alliance). On those systems, heating is tied to the associated pump (Pump 1 low-speed or a circulation pump), not to a free-running heater with no flow. That is Gecko controller-family behavior. Confirm whether your spa uses that platform.

### Low flow that only clears when a jet pump runs

Hot Spring troubleshooting links clogged filters to an open pressure switch that prevents heating until flow returns (Hot Spring). If circulation alone cannot satisfy the spa's flow requirement, some owners notice warming mainly when a stronger pump runs. Treat that as a flow problem to correct with manufacturer filter and water-level steps, not as permission to leave jets on for heat. Flow-related codes such as [Gecko FLO](/error-codes/gecko/flo) still require confirmation in your exact manual.

## What not to confuse

- Warmer-feeling water from jet mixing is not the same as confirmed heater operation
- Heater icons or mode lights mean what your manual says they mean
- One brand's Rest, Ready, Economy, or Sleep labels do not transfer to another brand
- Continuous jet use is not a supported heating method on this page

## Checks you can do safely

1. Note whether heat rises during programmed filtration, low-speed pump, high jets, or only one of those states.
2. Confirm Standard / Ready-style continuous heat versus Economy / filter-only heat in your manual.
3. Clean or inspect filters only as the manufacturer directs, and verify water level.
4. Compare the pattern with [Why Is My Hot Tub Taking So Long to Heat Up?](/troubleshooting/why-is-my-hot-tub-taking-so-long-to-heat-up) if the spa is still climbing from a cold start.

## When to call a technician

Call service if the spa never heats during normal filtration or circulation modes that your manual says should allow heat, if pressure-switch or flow faults keep returning, if conversion or heater-concurrent-with-jets behavior was changed incorrectly, or if electrical smell, smoke, or repeated trips appear.

## Bottom line

"Heats only when the jets are on" can reflect pump-linked heater logic, economy-style filter heating, or marginal flow, depending on the spa. Match the pattern to your manufacturer's documentation instead of forcing jets to run as a permanent heat source.
