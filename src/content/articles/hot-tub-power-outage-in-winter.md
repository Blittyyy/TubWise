## Direct answer

If your hot tub loses power in winter, **heating and powered circulation stop**. Active freeze-protection routines that run pumps or the heater need electricity and functioning equipment, so they cannot protect the spa while power is out. Keep a **properly fitted insulating cover closed** to help hold the heat already in the water. Treat a short outage as a monitoring situation; treat an extended outage as urgent—contact the spa manufacturer or a qualified technician promptly rather than improvising electrical workarounds.

## Safety note

Do not place space heaters, heat lamps, ceramic heaters, or work lights inside the spa cabinet. Do not use extension cords or homemade generator hookups for the spa. Do not casually drain a spa in freezing weather; water can remain trapped in plumbing and freeze. Do not restart equipment if you suspect frozen lines or visible freeze damage—get a professional assessment first. If the GFCI or breaker will not reset safely, leave power off and call qualified help. See also [Why Does My Hot Tub GFCI Keep Tripping?](/troubleshooting/why-does-my-hot-tub-gfci-keep-tripping).

## What stops when power is out

With no power:

- The heater cannot add heat
- Circulation and jet pumps cannot move water on command
- Control-driven freeze protection cannot run its normal protective cycles

That is different from a powered spa that is intentionally running freeze protection. For the powered case, see [Why Is My Hot Tub Running Freeze Protection?](/troubleshooting/why-is-my-hot-tub-running-freeze-protection).

## Why the cover matters

Manufacturer winter guidance emphasizes a good, snug, insulating cover to retain heat and limit heat loss (Hot Spring; Master Spas). During an outage, leaving the cover closed avoids dumping stored heat into cold air. Master Spas winter guidance stresses leaving the cover on and not opening it during a power outage (Master Spas).

A damaged, waterlogged, or poor-fitting cover retains heat less effectively. Cover condition guides: [When Should You Replace a Hot Tub Cover?](/maintenance/when-should-you-replace-a-hot-tub-cover) and [Why Is My Hot Tub Cover Sagging?](/maintenance/why-is-my-hot-tub-cover-sagging).

## Freezing risk is not one universal clock

How fast freeze damage becomes likely depends on outdoor temperature, wind, insulation, starting water temperature, cover quality, and how equipment and plumbing are arranged. Master Spas notes that the main water mass and the plumbing/equipment areas can face different freeze timing pressures (Master Spas). TubWise does **not** publish a universal number of hours or days before freezing occurs.

## Checklist: during the outage

1. Confirm the outage is real (home power vs spa-only). A blank spa display with the house still powered may be a different problem—see [Hot Tub Display Is Blank but the Breaker Is On](/troubleshooting/hot-tub-display-is-blank-but-the-breaker-is-on).
2. Keep the insulating cover closed and latched.
3. Do not open the spa “just to check” repeatedly; each opening dumps heat.
4. Note outdoor temperature and how long power has been off.
5. Do not add improvised heat sources in the cabinet or shell.

## Checklist: extended outage

1. Contact the spa manufacturer, dealer, or a qualified spa technician for model-specific guidance sooner rather than later.
2. Ask about professional options for your climate and spa construction—do not invent electrical workarounds.
3. If you must leave the property, tell a trusted person to monitor conditions without opening the spa unnecessarily.
4. Do not drain as a casual freeze strategy in deep cold unless a qualified professional directs a complete winterization that removes trapped line water.

## Checklist: when power returns

1. Restore power only through the spa’s normal disconnect / GFCI path. If protection devices will not reset, stop and call service.
2. Watch for error messages, unusual noises, or no flow after start-up.
3. Confirm water is circulating before expecting heat.
4. Check that temperature begins moving in the expected direction with the cover on; large unexpected drops after restoration can signal heat-loss or equipment issues—see [Why Does My Hot Tub Temperature Keep Dropping?](/troubleshooting/why-does-my-hot-tub-temperature-keep-dropping).
5. Verify water chemistry before soaking; outages interrupt normal sanitation routines.
6. If you suspect frozen plumbing, cracked fittings, or leaks after the freeze event, leave the system off and get a professional inspection before repeated heat or jet cycles.

For a planning estimate of warm-up time after water is circulating again, use the [Hot Tub Heat-Up Time Calculator](/calculators/hot-tub-heat-up-time-calculator). It does not predict freeze damage.
