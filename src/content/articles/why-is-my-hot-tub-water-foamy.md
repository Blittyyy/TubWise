Foamy hot tub water can be tied to soap and body-product residue that bathers and swimwear carry into the water, low calcium hardness, or water that has been in the spa for a while without a change. Jacuzzi and Hot Spring both document soap-based residue as a cause, and both also document low calcium hardness and accumulated dissolved solids as contributing factors. None of the sources reviewed for this article rank these causes by how often each one occurs, so treat them as documented possibilities to check rather than a most-likely-first list.

## Safety note

Foam by itself is not necessarily a sign of unsafe water, but it can appear alongside low sanitizer levels. Test your chlorine or bromine and pH before deciding whether the spa is safe to use. Follow the instructions on your chemical product labels, and don't mix different pool or spa chemicals together. For a closer look at whether foam signals a safety concern, see [Is Foamy Hot Tub Water Safe to Use?](/water-care/is-foamy-hot-tub-water-safe-to-use)

## Causes to check

### Soap, lotions, and body oils

Jacuzzi's guidance names a high concentration of lotions, body oils, and soap as a cause of foam, and recommends a spa defoamer along with a pH adjustment and sanitizer as the fix ([Jacuzzi](https://www.jacuzzi.com/en-us/Signs-and-Symptoms-Your-Hot-Tub-Water-Needs-Maintenance.html)). Hot Spring's owner's manual explains why this residue is hard to clear on its own: soap enters the water from bathers' bodies and from swimwear, and it isn't broken down by chlorine or bromine. Only ozone can oxidize soap, so foam inhibitors suppress the bubbles without removing the underlying soap ([Hot Spring](https://www.hotspring.com/wp-content/uploads/2023/07/highlife-owners-manual-en_us.pdf)). For a closer look at this specific source, see [Can Soap, Lotion, or Detergent Cause Hot Tub Foam?](/water-care/can-soap-lotion-or-detergent-cause-hot-tub-foam)

### Detergent residue from bathing suits

Jacuzzi's setup guide separately lists foamy water as a common setup-stage issue, describing it as soap residue from bathing suits, lotions, or cleaning products, and recommending a shower before use and avoiding soaps or bubble bath in the spa ([Jacuzzi](https://www.jacuzzi.com/en-us/Beginners-Guide-to-Setting-Up-Your-Jacuzzi.html)). A swimsuit rinsed in plain water rather than left with detergent residue is one of the simpler variables to rule out.

### Low calcium hardness

Both Jacuzzi and Hot Spring's guidance connect soft water (low calcium hardness) to foaming ([Jacuzzi](https://www.jacuzzi.com/en-us/Beginners-Guide-to-Setting-Up-Your-Jacuzzi.html); [Hot Spring](https://www.hotspring.com/wp-content/uploads/2023/07/highlife-owners-manual-en_us.pdf)). This is a water-chemistry cause rather than a contamination one, which is why testing calcium hardness alongside pH and sanitizer is worth doing before assuming the cause is bather products.

### Recently added chemicals

If the foam appeared right after you treated the water, the order or amount of product used can be a factor rather than a new source of contamination. See [Hot Tub Foam After Adding Chemicals: What Happened?](/water-care/hot-tub-foam-after-adding-chemicals) for that specific situation.

### Old water with accumulated soap or dissolved solids

Hot Spring's manual states that soap buildup eventually concentrates in the water to the point that it affects the feel of the water on skin, and that a drain and refill becomes the appropriate fix, with water life estimated at around four months depending on how much soap is introduced ([Hot Spring](https://www.hotspring.com/wp-content/uploads/2023/07/highlife-owners-manual-en_us.pdf)). That four-month figure comes from one manufacturer's guidance and isn't a universal chemistry threshold, so check your own owner's manual for a specific interval. This overlaps with cloudy-water causes tied to old water and total dissolved solids; see [Why Is My Hot Tub Water Cloudy?](/water-care/why-is-my-hot-tub-water-cloudy) if you're seeing haze along with the foam.

## Safe checks you can do

1. **Test the water first.** Check pH, alkalinity, calcium hardness, and sanitizer before adding anything. See [What to Look for in a Hot Tub Water Test Kit](/buying-guides/what-to-look-for-in-a-hot-tub-water-test-kit) if you need a kit that matches the parameters your system requires.
2. **Rinse swimwear in plain water** rather than leaving detergent residue on suits that go back into the spa.
3. **Shower before soaking** to reduce the lotions, oils, and soap residue bathers bring into the water.
4. **Use a spa-specific defoamer** for a short-term reduction in visible foam, following the product label.
5. **Adjust calcium hardness** if your test shows a low reading, following your product label and owner's manual target range.
6. **Consider how long the water has been in the spa.** Persistent foam despite correct chemistry and clean swimwear can point toward a drain and refill.

## When to stop and get help

Stop using the spa and don't just keep adding defoamer if:

- Sanitizer or pH readings are outside a safe range and you can't correct them
- Foam returns quickly after a defoamer treatment and a fresh water test
- You notice a strong chemical smell instead of little to no odor, which the CDC flags as a possible sign of a water problem ([CDC](https://www.cdc.gov/healthy-swimming/about/home-pool-and-hot-tub-water-treatment-and-testing.html))

If foam keeps returning despite correct chemistry, clean swimwear, and a defoamer, manufacturer guidance points to a drain and refill as the next step.
