## Direct answer

No. The cited manufacturers instruct owners to operate the spa with a filter installed at all times. Jacuzzi and Sundance both publish guidance against running a spa without a filter, including briefly, citing the risk that hair, debris, and oils that would normally be trapped can instead reach the pump, heater, and plumbing. TubWise did not find approved manufacturer guidance from either brand permitting routine operation without a filter, so this article does not describe one.

## Safety note

This is different from turning the spa off while the filter is out for cleaning, which both manufacturers recommend. The guidance below is about running the pump and jets with no filter installed at all, not about the filter being briefly removed while the power is off.

## What Jacuzzi and Sundance say

Jacuzzi's filter-care guide addresses this directly in its FAQ: "For safety and water quality, we advise against using the spa without any filter installed. Unfiltered operation allows contaminants to reach your pumps and heating elements" ([Jacuzzi](https://www.jacuzzi.com/en-us/Hot-Tub-Filter-Cleaning-Made-Easy.html)). Jacuzzi's suggested alternative, if you want the spa available during a deep clean, is to keep a second, already-clean cartridge on hand to swap in rather than running without one.

Sundance's guide is similarly direct: "never run your Sundance spa without a filter installed, even briefly. This risks sending debris directly into the circulation system and may void your warranty" ([Sundance Spas](https://www.sundancespas.com/blog-articles/how-to-clean-a-hot-tub-filter-using-sundance-r-spa-filters-the-right-way)). Sundance's FAQ repeats the point: the spa "should not be run or used without a filter installed," and recommends keeping the circulation completely off if the filter must be out for any length of time, reinstalling it before turning anything back on.

## What the manufacturers actually say, and what TubWise could not verify

Both manufacturers frame this as a risk to specific equipment, not just water clarity. Sundance describes debris flowing "directly into pumps, heaters, and plumbing," and Jacuzzi describes contaminants reaching "your pumps and heating elements." Sundance's guidance explicitly extends this instruction to brief use: "never run your Sundance spa without a filter installed, even briefly." Jacuzzi's guidance doesn't specify a duration; it states the company's advice against unfiltered operation without qualifying it by time.

TubWise did not find a source from either manufacturer that states how much time, if any, without a filter is considered low-risk, nor one confirming that a brief unfiltered run causes immediate or guaranteed damage. Neither claim is supported, so neither is made here. What is supported is the instruction itself: both manufacturers tell owners to keep a filter installed, and Sundance's instruction specifically covers brief use. Following the published instruction, rather than estimating an acceptable duration yourself, is the position this article takes.

Sundance separately connects this to warranty coverage: running the spa without a filter "may void your warranty." That's a distinct consideration from any mechanical risk, and it applies regardless of how much actual debris reaches the equipment during a given run.

It's also worth being clear about what these two sources don't address. Neither Jacuzzi's nor Sundance's published guidance describes a specific procedure, such as a chemical flush or a leak-sealant treatment, where running without a filter is presented as manufacturer-approved. Where that kind of guidance exists for a particular product, it would come from that product's own instructions, not from general hot tub filter-care pages, and TubWise did not find that guidance from Jacuzzi or Sundance for their spas.

## What manufacturers recommend instead

- **Turn off the spa before removing the filter**, and keep it off for the entire time the filter is out, whether you're rinsing it, soaking it, or waiting on a replacement.
- **Keep a second filter cartridge on hand.** Both Jacuzzi and Sundance suggest this as the practical way to keep the spa usable during a deep clean, by swapping in the clean spare rather than running without one.
- **Reinstall the filter before restoring power**, following the process in [How to Clean a Hot Tub Filter](/maintenance/how-to-clean-a-hot-tub-filter).
- **Replace a damaged or worn-out filter promptly** rather than leaving the spa without one while you decide. See [When Should You Replace a Hot Tub Filter?](/maintenance/when-should-you-replace-a-hot-tub-filter).

## Safe checks the homeowner can do

1. **Confirm the pump is off** any time the filter compartment is open, whether for cleaning, inspection, or replacement.
2. **Keep a spare filter cartridge** so you're not tempted to run the spa while your primary filter is soaking or drying. See [How Often Should You Clean a Hot Tub Filter?](/maintenance/how-often-should-you-clean-a-hot-tub-filter) for how long a soak typically takes.
3. **Check your own owner's manual** for any spa-specific guidance, since manufacturer instructions can differ and yours should take priority.
4. **Order a replacement filter promptly** if your current one is damaged, rather than operating without one in the meantime. See [When Should You Replace a Hot Tub Filter?](/maintenance/when-should-you-replace-a-hot-tub-filter) for the signs it's time to replace rather than clean.

## When to stop and call a technician

- You've been operating the spa without a filter, even briefly, and now notice reduced or weak flow (see [Why Are My Hot Tub Jets Weak?](/troubleshooting/why-are-my-hot-tub-jets-weak)), unusual pump noise, or debris in the plumbing
- You don't have a working filter and can't get a replacement quickly, and the spa needs to run for another reason, such as freeze protection in cold weather
- You're unsure whether debris may have already reached the pump or heater

If you have questions specific to your situation, especially around winter freeze-protection cycles, your manufacturer's guidance or a qualified technician is the appropriate source, not general assumptions about what's safe for a short time.
