Scale on a hot tub shell is mineral deposit, not an oil-and-dirt scum ring. Hot Spring's troubleshooting chart lists **Scale** with total alkalinity and/or pH too high and high calcium content in the water ([Hot Spring](https://www.hotspring.com/owners/water-care-and-troubleshooting)). The Highlife Collection owner's manual defines scale as rough calcium-bearing deposits that can coat spa surfaces, heaters, and plumbing lines, and clog filters, and states that scaling is generally caused by mineral content combined with high pH, forming more readily at higher water temperatures ([Hot Spring Highlife Manual](https://www.hotspring.com/wp-content/uploads/2023/07/highlife-owners-manual-en_us.pdf)).

Jacuzzi describes calcium carbonate scale as white, chalky buildup that can clog jets and affect heaters when high alkalinity drives pH high ([Jacuzzi](https://www.jacuzzi.com/en-us/How-to-Lower-Alkalinity-in-Hot-Tub.html)). Sundance Spas describes scaling as a sandpaper-like roughness on spa surfaces from calcium buildup ([Sundance Spas](https://www.sundancespas.com/blog-articles/overcoming-hard-water-issues-in-your-hot-tub-3-steps-to-combat-scaling)). If the main complaint is texture rather than a visible crust, start with [Why Does My Hot Tub Surface Feel Rough or Sandy?](/water-care/why-does-my-hot-tub-surface-feel-rough-or-sandy). For fill precautions in hard-water areas, see [How to Fill a Hot Tub With Hard Water](/water-care/how-to-fill-a-hot-tub-with-hard-water).

For soft brownish film at the waterline, see [Why Is There a Ring Around My Hot Tub Waterline?](/water-care/why-is-there-a-ring-around-my-hot-tub-waterline). For rust-colored metal stains, see [Why Does My Hot Tub Have Rust-Colored Stains?](/water-care/why-does-my-hot-tub-have-rust-colored-stains).

## Safety note

Use only cleaners approved for your spa shell. Do not scrape acrylic with metal tools or abrasive pads that your manual forbids. See [How to Clean a Hot Tub Shell Without Damaging It](/maintenance/how-to-clean-a-hot-tub-shell-without-damaging-it). Follow chemical product labels. Do not mix different pool or spa chemicals. Confirm calcium, pH, and alkalinity targets in your own manual; Hot Spring publishes different hardness ranges for standard care versus its FreshWater Salt System context ([Hot Spring Highlife Manual](https://www.hotspring.com/wp-content/uploads/2023/07/highlife-owners-manual-en_us.pdf)).

## Causes to check

### High calcium content

Hot Spring's Scale chart row lists high calcium content in the water ([Hot Spring](https://www.hotspring.com/owners/water-care-and-troubleshooting)). The Highlife manual states that high calcium levels can cause scale formation on spa equipment and shell, and that low calcium can make water corrosive ([Hot Spring Highlife Manual](https://www.hotspring.com/wp-content/uploads/2023/07/highlife-owners-manual-en_us.pdf)). Hot Spring's calcium FAQ ties reducing calcium hardness to protecting jet pumps and heaters from scale buildup ([Hot Spring](https://www.hotspring.com/faq/how-do-i-reduce-calcium-hardness-my-spa-water)).

### High total alkalinity and/or high pH

The same Hot Spring Scale row lists total alkalinity and/or pH too high ([Hot Spring](https://www.hotspring.com/owners/water-care-and-troubleshooting)). The Highlife manual states that high pH can cause high levels of calcium to form scale ([Hot Spring Highlife Manual](https://www.hotspring.com/wp-content/uploads/2023/07/highlife-owners-manual-en_us.pdf)). Jacuzzi links calcium carbonate scale formation to high alkalinity and notes that scaling accelerates when high alkalinity drives pH above 8.0 in that guidance ([Jacuzzi](https://www.jacuzzi.com/en-us/How-to-Lower-Alkalinity-in-Hot-Tub.html)). Sundance likewise names elevated alkalinity among contributors to calcium buildup ([Sundance Spas](https://www.sundancespas.com/blog-articles/overcoming-hard-water-issues-in-your-hot-tub-3-steps-to-combat-scaling)).

### Hard water and topping off

Hot Spring notes that calcium hardness can increase over time from topping off ([Hot Spring](https://www.hotspring.com/faq/how-do-i-reduce-calcium-hardness-my-spa-water)). The Highlife manual notes that as water evaporates and new water is added, dissolved minerals including calcium increase ([Hot Spring Highlife Manual](https://www.hotspring.com/wp-content/uploads/2023/07/highlife-owners-manual-en_us.pdf)).

### Scale versus other deposits

- **Waterline oils and dirt** are Hot Spring's organic buildup / scum ring path, not the Scale row ([Hot Spring](https://www.hotspring.com/owners/water-care-and-troubleshooting)).
- **Green or brown metal stains** belong on the Stains path with iron or copper ([Hot Spring](https://www.hotspring.com/owners/water-care-and-troubleshooting)).

## Safe checks you can do

1. **Test calcium hardness, total alkalinity, and pH first.** See [How Often Should You Test Hot Tub Water?](/maintenance/how-often-should-you-test-hot-tub-water).
2. **Compare readings to your manual.** Do not borrow another brand's numeric target without checking your own documentation.
3. **Lower alkalinity and pH only as labeled** if they are high for your system. Hot Spring's Scale row directs owners to adjust total alkalinity and pH into range ([Hot Spring](https://www.hotspring.com/owners/water-care-and-troubleshooting)).
4. **Address high calcium with the method your brand documents.** Hot Spring's calcium FAQ describes testing, then using a Vanishing Act calcium remover when needed, and considering a water softener when calcium is very high ([Hot Spring](https://www.hotspring.com/faq/how-do-i-reduce-calcium-hardness-my-spa-water)). Follow that FAQ and your owner's manual for placement and timing. Do not invent a dose for a different brand's product.
5. **If scale already coats the shell, drain and clean per your brand.** Hot Spring's Scale row says to drain, scrub off the scale, clean the spa shell with FreshWater Spa Shine, and refill when scale requires removal ([Hot Spring](https://www.hotspring.com/owners/water-care-and-troubleshooting)). Match cleaners to your shell guidance.
6. **Retest after corrections** before assuming the problem is solved.

## When to stop and get help

Stop scrubbing and stop stacking calcium removers if:

- Scale returns quickly after labeled chemistry corrections
- You cannot bring calcium, alkalinity, or pH into your manual's ranges
- The shell is scratched or dull after cleaning attempts
- Flow, heating, or filter problems continue after a drain and clean

Contact your dealer or a qualified spa technician. For drain interval context, see [How Often Should You Drain and Refill a Hot Tub?](/maintenance/how-often-should-you-drain-and-refill-a-hot-tub).
