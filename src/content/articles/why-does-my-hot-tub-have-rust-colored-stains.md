Rust-colored marks on a hot tub shell are not automatic proof that a metal part inside the spa is corroding. Hot Spring's troubleshooting chart lists **Stains** with total alkalinity and/or pH too low, and high iron or copper in source water, with solutions that include adjusting alkalinity or pH and using a metal deposit inhibitor ([Hot Spring](https://www.hotspring.com/owners/water-care-and-troubleshooting)). Hot Spring manuals also state that high iron or copper content in the water may produce green or brown stains on the spa ([Hot Spring Highlife Manual](https://www.hotspring.com/wp-content/uploads/2023/07/highlife-owners-manual-en_us.pdf)). Homeowners often describe those brown metal stains as rust-colored.

This article is about shell and surface staining. For brown color throughout the water, see [Why Is My Hot Tub Water Brown?](/water-care/why-is-my-hot-tub-water-brown). For chalky mineral scale rather than metal staining, see [Why Is There Scale on My Hot Tub Shell?](/water-care/why-is-there-scale-on-my-hot-tub-shell).

## Safety note

Use only cleaners approved for your spa shell. Harsh household cleaners and abrasives can damage acrylic. See [How to Clean a Hot Tub Shell Without Damaging It](/maintenance/how-to-clean-a-hot-tub-shell-without-damaging-it). Follow chemical product labels. Do not mix different pool or spa chemicals. If you treat for metals with FreshWater Stain & Scale in a Hot Spring water-care context, Hot Spring's metals FAQ directs owners to wait 24 hours before adding chlorine or other oxidizers ([Hot Spring](https://www.hotspring.com/faq/how-do-i-reduce-metals-my-water)). Confirm amounts and wait times on the product label and your manual.

## Causes to check

### Iron or copper in source water

Hot Spring's Stains chart row lists high iron or copper in source water ([Hot Spring](https://www.hotspring.com/owners/water-care-and-troubleshooting)). The Highlife Collection owner's manual warns that iron and copper can stain the spa shell if left unchecked and that high iron or copper may produce green or brown stains ([Hot Spring Highlife Manual](https://www.hotspring.com/wp-content/uploads/2023/07/highlife-owners-manual-en_us.pdf)). The same manual's fill guidance says the Clean Screen pre-filter removes contaminants such as rust, dirt, detergents, and algae from fill water, along with dissolved metals including copper, iron, and manganese.

That "rust" language in the fill-filter list describes material removed from fill water. It is not a diagnosis that an internal heater or fitting is rusting.

### Low total alkalinity and/or low pH

The same Hot Spring Stains row lists total alkalinity and/or pH too low as a probable cause alongside metals ([Hot Spring](https://www.hotspring.com/owners/water-care-and-troubleshooting)). Test before adjusting. Target ranges vary by brand and product line, so confirm targets in your own manual.

### Stains versus a waterline scum ring

An oil-and-dirt scum ring at the waterline is a different Hot Spring chart row from Stains ([Hot Spring](https://www.hotspring.com/owners/water-care-and-troubleshooting)). Soft brownish film that wipes off with a clean rag may be organic buildup. Colored staining that returns after wiping is closer to the metals path. See [Why Is There a Ring Around My Hot Tub Waterline?](/water-care/why-is-there-a-ring-around-my-hot-tub-waterline).

### Stains versus calcium scale

Scale is linked to high calcium and high total alkalinity and/or pH on Hot Spring's Scale row ([Hot Spring](https://www.hotspring.com/owners/water-care-and-troubleshooting)). Hard, chalky white or gray deposits are a different problem from rust-colored metal stains. See [Why Is There Scale on My Hot Tub Shell?](/water-care/why-is-there-scale-on-my-hot-tub-shell).

## Safe checks you can do

1. **Describe the mark.** Soft wipe-away film, hard chalky crust, or colored stain that remains after wiping.
2. **Test total alkalinity, pH, and sanitizer.** Hot Spring's Stains row starts with chemistry and metals ([Hot Spring](https://www.hotspring.com/owners/water-care-and-troubleshooting)).
3. **Ask about fill water and pre-filtration.** Hot Spring points owners with metals toward Clean Screen and dealer guidance ([Hot Spring](https://www.hotspring.com/faq/how-do-i-reduce-metals-my-water)).
4. **Clean only with shell-approved products.** Follow [How to Clean a Hot Tub Shell Without Damaging It](/maintenance/how-to-clean-a-hot-tub-shell-without-damaging-it). Do not scrub metal stains with banned household cleaners.
5. **If metals are confirmed or strongly suspected, follow labeled metal treatment.** Hot Spring's metals FAQ describes Clean Screen fill, FreshWater Stain & Scale, and a 24-hour wait before chlorine or other oxidizers ([Hot Spring](https://www.hotspring.com/faq/how-do-i-reduce-metals-my-water)). Use the amounts on your product label.
6. **Retest after any required wait time** before soaking.

## When to stop and get help

Stop scrubbing and stop stacking metal treatments if:

- Stains remain after approved cleaners and a labeled metal treatment for your brand
- The shell shows dulling, scratches, or soft spots after cleaning attempts
- Brown bulk water continues after chemistry and metal checks ([Why Is My Hot Tub Water Brown?](/water-care/why-is-my-hot-tub-water-brown))
- You cannot bring alkalinity and pH into the ranges your manual requires

Contact your dealer or a qualified spa technician for persistent metal staining or unknown well-water metal content. Color that appears right after a refill also has a fill-water path; see [Why Is My Hot Tub Water Discolored After Filling?](/water-care/why-is-my-hot-tub-water-discolored-after-filling).
