## Direct answer

If the topside does nothing useful, Hot Spring's troubleshooting chart treats an inoperative spa as a power failure, a tripped GFCI, a tripped heater high-limit thermostat, or an activated spa lock (Hot Spring). Confirm which of those manufacturer-documented cases you are in before assuming the panel itself failed.

## Safety note

Do not open the control box. Do not touch wiring. Do not measure voltage, resistance, or continuity. Do not bypass a GFCI, high-limit device, or other safety switch. Reset a breaker or GFCI once only if your owner's manual permits it. If it will not reset, or trips again, stop and contact qualified service. Hot Spring warns that a GFCI that will not test or reset correctly can signal an electrical malfunction and shock risk (Hot Spring Limelight owner's manual).

## Separate four different problems

These look similar from the patio but are not the same repair path:

1. **No usable power at the spa.** Jacuzzi tells owners who see no power to confirm the main breaker and spa GFCI are on, and to check that an exterior disconnect has not been turned off (Jacuzzi).
2. **Blank display while the house breaker looks on.** That pattern has its own check order in [Hot Tub Display Is Blank but the Breaker Is On](/troubleshooting/hot-tub-display-is-blank-but-the-breaker-is-on).
3. **Display lit, but buttons or touch do nothing.** Spa lock and wireless-panel communication issues belong in [Why Are My Hot Tub Buttons Not Responding?](/troubleshooting/why-are-my-hot-tub-buttons-not-responding).
4. **Flashing indicators or dashes instead of a normal temperature.** Those patterns are scoped by brand and manual in [Hot Tub Control Panel Is Flashing or Showing Dashes](/troubleshooting/hot-tub-control-panel-is-flashing-or-showing-dashes).

This article is the umbrella triage: decide which case you have, then follow the safe checks that match.

## Manufacturer-documented causes of an inoperative spa

### Power failure

Hot Spring lists a power-source problem as the first cause when the entire spa is inoperative, with the fix stated as checking the power source (Hot Spring; Hot Spring Limelight owner's manual). Jacuzzi's electrical guide likewise starts with the main breaker, spa GFCI, and exterior disconnect when there is no power (Jacuzzi).

### Tripped GFCI

Hot Spring lists a tripped GFCI as a cause of an inoperative spa and instructs owners to reset it, then call for service if it will not reset (Hot Spring). Jacuzzi advises against repeatedly resetting a GFCI that trips immediately and treats that pattern as a reason to call a licensed electrician (Jacuzzi).

### Tripped heater high-limit thermostat

Hot Spring lists a tripped heater high-limit thermostat among inoperative-spa causes. The documented homeowner step is to disconnect power for about 30 seconds to allow the high-limit circuit to reset, then check for clogged filters if the trip continues (Hot Spring; Hot Spring Limelight owner's manual). A flashing Power indicator on certain Hot Spring panels is documented as a sign that the high-limit has tripped (Hot Spring Limelight owner's manual).

If the spa keeps shutting down after that reset path, see [Why Does My Hot Tub Keep Turning Off?](/troubleshooting/why-does-my-hot-tub-keep-turning-off).

### Spa lock activated

Hot Spring lists spa lock as a cause of an inoperative spa and tells owners to deactivate it (Hot Spring). On Limelight models, spa lock disables the controls on the control panel, and the jet pumps will not function while spa lock is on (Hot Spring Limelight owner's manual). Follow your own manual's unlock steps. Do not treat spa lock as proof that the panel hardware failed.

## Safe checks the homeowner can do

1. **Look at the whole spa, not only the topside.** Note whether lights, jets, or status indicators still react.
2. **Confirm the spa disconnect and GFCI positions** against your manual and Jacuzzi's no-power checks (Jacuzzi).
3. **If your manual allows one reset,** restore power once and watch whether the panel returns.
4. **Check for a spa-lock or lock icon** and deactivate it only by the method in your manual (Hot Spring).
5. **If a Power or Ready indicator is blinking,** treat that as a documented diagnostic clue, not a license to open the equipment pack (Hot Spring).
6. **Clean or inspect the filter only if your manual ties that step to a high-limit reset path,** then stop if the trip returns (Hot Spring).

## When to stop and call a technician

- The GFCI or breaker will not reset, or trips again after one attempt
- Power is confirmed at the disconnect, but the spa stays fully dark
- High-limit or similar protective trips return after the filter and flow checks your manual allows
- You would need to open the control box, test wiring, or replace topside hardware to continue
- The panel shows a brand-specific code, dashes, or Watchdog-style message you cannot match in your own manual

For heating that fails while the panel still works, use [Hot Tub Not Heating: What to Check First](/troubleshooting/hot-tub-not-heating) instead of this control-panel path.
