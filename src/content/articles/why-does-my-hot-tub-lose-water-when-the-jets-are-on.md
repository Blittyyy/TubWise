## Direct answer

Water that disappears mainly when the jets run can be splash and overflow from a high waterline, or a leak that shows when the pump pressurizes the plumbing. Those are different problems. Hot Spot Collection manuals tell owners to activate the pump after seating pump unions and confirm the fittings do not leak (Hot Spring Hot Spot Collection guide). Jacuzzi separately links pump and heater leaks, and worn jet or plumbing seals, to water loss (Jacuzzi). Rule out overflow first, then look for wet fittings with a careful power-off check.

## Safety note

Turn power off before opening the equipment bay (Jacuzzi). Do not run the spa with the equipment door removed if your manufacturer forbids it (Hot Spring Hot Spot Collection guide). Do not open the control box. Do not pressure-test plumbing with tools your manual does not document. Stop if water reaches electrical components.

## Pattern A: splash, overflow, or high water

Jacuzzi describes keeping water just above the skimmer intake as part of stable operation (Jacuzzi). Hot Spot Collection manuals also link weak or surging jets to a low water condition, which is the opposite problem, but both extremes matter for jet use (Hot Spring Hot Spot Collection guide).

If the shell was filled high, jets can throw water over the rim or into the cover hinge area. That can look like a leak after a soak even though the plumbing is intact.

Overflow-leaning clues:

- Water on the cover top, deck, or rim after jet use
- No dampness inside the equipment bay
- Level stabilizes when the spa sits idle with the cover on

If the spa loses water overnight with jets off, use [Hot Tub Losing Water Overnight: Leak or Evaporation?](/troubleshooting/hot-tub-losing-water-overnight-leak-or-evaporation) instead of assuming a pressurized fitting failed.

## Pattern B: leak that appears under pump pressure

Some leaks show more clearly when pumps run. On Hot Spot Collection spas, Watkins's priming steps include activating the pump after hand-tightening a pump union and checking that the union is tight enough to keep it from leaking (Hot Spring Hot Spot Collection guide). That is manufacturer language for a leak check under pump operation on that product line.

Jacuzzi lists pump or heater leaks among water-level causes and tells owners to inspect wet spots near plumbing connections (Jacuzzi). Jacuzzi also states that worn seals around jets and plumbing connections can cause leaks, and that leaks under the pump can signal pump-seal failure needing professional repair (Jacuzzi).

Pressure-leaning clues:

- Fresh wetness at unions, heater connections, or under the pump after jets run
- Level drops during jet use even when the rim is not overflowing
- Equipment bay was dry until pumps ran

Continue location-specific checks in [Hot Tub Leaking From Underneath: What to Check](/troubleshooting/hot-tub-leaking-from-underneath).

## What this is not

- Not proof the heater element failed
- Not proof every overnight drop is the same issue
- Not an invitation to dig foam or replace shaft seals at home
- Not the same topic as weak jets with normal water volume; for flow strength, see [Why Are My Hot Tub Jets Weak?](/troubleshooting/why-are-my-hot-tub-jets-weak)

## Safe checks the homeowner can do

1. **Mark the waterline** before running jets.
2. **Run jets briefly** and watch for rim overflow or cover splash.
3. **Power off**, open equipment access only as your manual allows, and look for new wetness at pump and heater plumbing.
4. **On a Hot Spot Collection spa with matching manual language**, confirm equipment-compartment unions are hand-tight, then use the manual's pump-on leak check if you are comfortable with that documented step.
5. **Lower an overfilled spa** to the manual fill line if splash was the only wetness you found.
6. **Call service** for seal replacement, persistent union weep, or any wet electrical parts.

## When to stop and call a technician

- Water appears at the pump wet end or heater connections and keeps returning
- You cannot tell overflow from a plumbing leak
- Finding the source needs foam removal, shell work, or seal kits
- Water is near the control pack or motor electrical end
- The pump is also unusually loud; see [Why Is My Hot Tub Pump Making Noise?](/troubleshooting/why-is-my-hot-tub-pump-making-noise)

For the wider leak-versus-evaporation decision when the spa is idle, start with [Why Is My Hot Tub Leaking?](/troubleshooting/why-is-my-hot-tub-leaking).
