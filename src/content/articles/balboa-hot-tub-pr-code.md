## Direct answer

**Pr** on documented Balboa topside panels means the spa is in **priming mode** after power-up. Balboa’s own message guidance describes priming as a start-up state that lets you run pumps and confirm water is flowing while the heater stays off. It is **not** inherently a fault code.

Newer Balboa TP-series and Spa Touch guides often show the words **Priming Mode** (Spa Touch documentation also references message **M019**) instead of the two-letter **Pr** display used on many legacy ML-style panels. Confirm the exact wording in your spa brand’s manual for your panel.

## Safety note

Do not exit priming mode until you can verify water is moving through the jets as your controller guide describes. Do not let a pump run without priming for more than the time your Balboa guide allows. Balboa TP7 and Spa Touch fill guides state a pump should not run without priming for more than **2 minutes**, and under no circumstances beyond the end of the **4-minute** Priming Mode (Balboa). Do not loosen pump unions, open electrical enclosures, or bypass flow or heater protection. If pumps cannot establish flow, shut the spa off and call a qualified spa technician.

## Why priming happens after startup

After a fill—or anytime the system is powered up—air can remain trapped in plumbing. Balboa defines priming as removing that trapped air. Priming is complete when water flows from the jets without air bubbles (Balboa TP7 / Spa Touch fill guides).

Balboa instructs owners to open valves and jets before filling so air can escape, then to prime pumps after power-on. A recent drain-and-refill makes priming especially important because new air often enters the lines. Related TubWise guides: [Hot Tub Not Heating After a Refill](/troubleshooting/hot-tub-not-heating-after-a-refill) and [How to Tell if a Hot Tub Has an Air Lock](/troubleshooting/how-to-tell-if-a-hot-tub-has-an-air-lock).

## Why heating is disabled during priming

On the Balboa systems documented in the TP7, Spa Touch 3, and ML553 start-up material cited here, the heater is disabled during Priming Mode so the system does not energize the heater under low-flow or no-flow conditions (Balboa). Automatic heating and filtering resume after Priming Mode ends.

So a spa that “won’t heat” while **Pr** / Priming Mode is showing may still be behaving as designed. Wait for priming to finish successfully before judging heater performance.

## Normal vs abnormal observations

| Observation | More consistent with | What to do |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Pr / Priming Mode appears right after power-up | Normal start-up on documented Balboa systems | Follow your panel’s priming steps; watch jets for bubble-free flow |
| Session lasts about 4 minutes, then normal operation begins | Documented Priming Mode length on TP7, Spa Touch, and ML553 guides | Allow the automatic exit, or exit early only after pumps are primed per your manual |
| Display shows dashes briefly after priming ends | Documented TP7 / Spa Touch behavior while the system samples heater water temperature | Wait the short flow interval your guide describes before expecting a temperature reading |
| Jets move water without air after priming each pump | Successful prime | Proceed to normal heating/filtering |
| Pump runs with little or no jet flow, loud cavitation, or repeated failed primes | Failure to establish flow | Stop the pump within the guide’s time limits; shut power off and call service if flow cannot be established |
| You clear Priming Mode while jets still spit only air | Unsafe early exit | Do not keep trying to heat; restore flow first |

Timing details above are scoped to the Balboa guides cited on this page. Other spa brands that install Balboa hardware may print different topside labels or overlay text.

## How documented Balboa priming sessions work

On Spa Touch 3 and TP7 fill guides:

- Priming Mode follows the start-up splash screen.
- Only pump controls are available for priming; the heater stays off.
- Priming Mode lasts **4 minutes**, or you can exit earlier after pumps are primed using the control path your panel guide shows (Back on Spa Touch / TP7 fill screens; Temp on the TP7 messages page for that family).
- Cycling power starts a new priming session if you need another attempt (Balboa).
- Momentarily turning a pump off and on can help priming; Balboa says not to do that more than **5 times** (Balboa).

On the ML553 user guide, start-up enters Priming mode for up to **4 minutes**, then the spa begins heating in Standard mode; Cool or Warm can exit Priming mode early (Balboa). Related Balboa service messaging for the **Pr** display states that priming is not an error message and that the heater will not start during that period.

Always use the priming button sequence printed for **your** topside. Do not invent union-bleeding steps that your spa brand does not authorize for homeowners.

## Pr vs dry-heater or flow warnings

**Pr / Priming Mode** is a start-up priming state.

**Heater may be dry / Heater is dry** (legacy **dr** / **drY** on some panels) are heater-protection messages about inadequate water in or through the heater. See [Balboa Hot Tub dr and dry Error Codes](/error-codes/balboa/dr-dry).

A spa can finish Priming Mode and still later show a dry-heater or low-flow message if circulation through the heater is inadequate. Priming success and heater-flow protection are related, but they are not the same display.

After priming, some panels show dashes while temperature is unknown. That pattern is covered in [Hot Tub Control Panel Is Flashing or Showing Dashes](/troubleshooting/hot-tub-control-panel-is-flashing-or-showing-dashes).

## What to check in your controller manual

1. Confirm the pack/panel family without opening electrical boxes.
2. Find the Priming Mode / **Pr** section in the Balboa guide or spa-brand reprint that matches your panel.
3. Follow only the owner priming steps listed there.
4. If pumps will not prime within the documented limits, power down and call service.
