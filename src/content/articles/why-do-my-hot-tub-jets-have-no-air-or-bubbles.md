## Direct answer

If the jets push a solid stream of water but you get little or no air mix, start with the air controls, not the pump. Hot Spot Collection manuals describe an air-control valve that sends air volume to the jets, and they tell owners to close that control when the spa is idle (Hot Spot Collection owner's manual). Jacuzzi J-200 manuals likewise treat air controls as adjustable devices that can be closed during filtration (Jacuzzi). If the water stream itself is weak, this is the wrong page.

## Safety note

Shut power off before removing an air-control knob or valve body. Do not force a seized control. Do not open the equipment pack to chase air lines unless your manual documents a homeowner-accessible step. Stay on topside observation and manufacturer-supported valve service only.

## What air controls do

Air controls open a path that lets the jet venturi pull air into the water stream. Open usually means more bubbles and a livelier feel. Closed means more of a solid water jet and quieter operation. Hot Spot explicitly links closing air controls to quieter covered operation (Hot Spot Collection owner's manual). Jacuzzi's filtration note to close air controls where present is the same idea from the other direction: air mix is optional and adjustable (Jacuzzi).

If the noise while jets are on is your main concern, see [Why Does My Hot Tub Make Noise When the Jets Are On?](/troubleshooting/why-does-my-hot-tub-make-noise-when-the-jets-are-on). That page separates expected air sound from warning pump noise.

## Safe checks the homeowner can do

1. **Confirm water flow is actually strong** before chasing air. No air page cannot fix a starved pump.
2. **Find the air controls.** They are often smaller topside knobs than the large water diverter.
3. **Open each air control fully** and note whether the jet sound and feel change.
4. **Close them fully** and confirm the opposite change. Hot Spot documents that closed air reduces the air volume sent to the jets (Hot Spot Collection owner's manual).
5. **Check whether every air-assisted jet is flat, or only one area.** A single flat zone can still be a local air control, a closed jet face, or a diverter position.
6. **If the knob will not turn, stop and shut power off.** Do not pry it. Follow only the air-control service steps printed for your brand.
7. **If the no-air symptom started right after a drain and refill and water flow is also uneven,** confirm air lock before rebuilding valves.

## When to stop and call a technician

- Air controls turn but never change jet feel or sound
- The valve is cracked, leaks into the spa, or spins without engaging
- Only some air-assisted jets stay dead after open/close tests and diverter checks
- You would need to open the cabinet and rebuild hidden air lines to continue

Manufacturer air-control service differs by brand and year. Use your own parts diagram rather than assuming every knob rebuilds the same way.
