Not always, and not as a first step. Manufacturer guidance treats draining as one of several possible fixes for cloudy water — used after chemistry has been corrected and the filter is clean, not instead of checking those things first. Draining does make sense when cloudiness persists despite correct treatment, or when the water is old enough that dissolved mineral content (TDS) may have built up beyond the level a manufacturer recommends.

## Safety note

Cloudy water can be more than a look-and-feel issue. The CDC lists unusually cloudy water as a warning sign of possible elevated germ levels ([CDC](https://www.cdc.gov/healthy-swimming/prevention/index.html)). If you're not confident the water is safe and can't quickly confirm sanitizer and pH are correct, stay out of the spa while you work through the checks below.

## When manufacturers point toward draining

### Cloudiness persists despite correct treatment

Jacuzzi's troubleshooting guidance is direct: "Sometimes, the best solution for persistent cloudy water is to drain and refill your hot tub," and lists ongoing cloudiness despite treatment as a sign it may be time to do so ([Jacuzzi](https://www.jacuzzi.com/en-us/Troubleshooting-Cloudy-Hot-Tub-Water.html)). The key word is *despite* — this applies after you've already corrected chemistry and cleaned the filter, not as a substitute for doing so.

### High total dissolved solids (TDS)

Jacuzzi ties persistent cloudiness to high total dissolved solids, and its setup guide recommends a full drain and refill every 3–4 months, or sooner if TDS exceeds 2,000 ppm ([Jacuzzi](https://www.jacuzzi.com/en-us/Beginners-Guide-to-Setting-Up-Your-Jacuzzi.html)). TDS builds up gradually from minerals, chemical byproducts, and everything else dissolved in the water over time. Manufacturer guidance points to draining and refilling when dissolved solids have built up beyond the recommended level, rather than to filtration or shock treatment. If you have a TDS-capable test kit or strip, a high reading is a more objective signal than appearance alone.

### It's been a while since the last drain

Hot Spring's own troubleshooting chart lists "drain, clean and refill the spa" as one of several solutions for cloudy water, alongside chemistry and filter fixes ([Hot Spring](https://www.hotspring.com/owners/water-care-and-troubleshooting)). If you can't remember when the spa was last drained, or it's been longer than your manufacturer's recommended interval, old water is worth ruling out as a possible explanation.

## When draining isn't the first step

Manufacturer troubleshooting sequences check chemistry and the filter before recommending a drain, not after. If you haven't yet:

- Tested and corrected pH, alkalinity, and sanitizer
- Cleaned or replaced the filter — see [Can a Dirty Filter Make Hot Tub Water Cloudy?](/water-care/can-a-dirty-filter-make-hot-tub-water-cloudy)
- Given the water a full circulation cycle after those corrections — see [How Long Does It Take Cloudy Hot Tub Water to Clear?](/water-care/how-long-does-it-take-cloudy-hot-tub-water-to-clear)

...it's worth doing those first. If the cloudiness started right after you added chemicals, see [Hot Tub Water Cloudy After Adding Chemicals](/water-care/hot-tub-water-cloudy-after-adding-chemicals) before assuming a drain is needed — the cause can be the order or amount of product used, not the water itself. Draining and refilling a spa takes time and, depending on your water source, may need a fresh round of startup chemicals — it's a reasonable fallback, not a shortcut around basic troubleshooting.

Jacuzzi's setup guide also recommends testing your fill water source before adding any chemicals, since well water can need different treatment than municipal tap water, and using a hose filter attachment when refilling to reduce metals and minerals going into the spa ([Jacuzzi](https://www.jacuzzi.com/en-us/Beginners-Guide-to-Setting-Up-Your-Jacuzzi.html)). Draining doesn't fix a source-water problem on its own if the same untreated water goes right back in.

## Safe checks the homeowner can do

1. **Rule out chemistry and filter causes first**, using the checks in [Why Is My Hot Tub Water Cloudy?](/water-care/why-is-my-hot-tub-water-cloudy)
2. **Check a TDS reading if you can.** A high result supports draining over further chemical treatment.
3. **Note how long it's been since the last drain.** Compare it against your owner's manual's recommended interval.
4. **If you decide to drain,** follow your owner's manual for the shutoff, drain, and refill procedure, and clean the shell before refilling.
5. **Use a hose filter attachment when refilling** if you have one, to reduce the metals and minerals introduced from your tap water.
6. **Test your fill water before adding chemicals.** Well water and municipal tap water can need different treatment, so a baseline reading helps avoid over- or under-dosing from the start.
7. **Rebalance from scratch after refilling** — total alkalinity, then pH, then sanitizer — rather than assuming old settings still apply to fresh water.

## When to stop and call a technician

Draining and refilling is a normal homeowner maintenance task, but call a technician instead if:

- The water is cloudy again shortly after a full drain and refill with correct chemistry
- You notice unusual smells, discoloration, or debris that a drain doesn't resolve
- You're unsure how to safely access or operate your spa's drain valve, or your manual directs you to a technician for this model

Persistent problems after a full drain point toward equipment or plumbing rather than the water itself, and are best handled by qualified service.
