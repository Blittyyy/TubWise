A rough or sandy feel on seats, walls, or the waterline is a **surface** cue. Sundance Spas describes scaling as an unexpected roughness akin to sandpaper on the spa's surface from calcium buildup ([Sundance Spas](https://www.sundancespas.com/blog-articles/overcoming-hard-water-issues-in-your-hot-tub-3-steps-to-combat-scaling)). Hot Spring defines scale as rough calcium-bearing deposits that can coat spa surfaces ([Hot Spring Highlife Manual](https://www.hotspring.com/wp-content/uploads/2023/07/highlife-owners-manual-en_us.pdf)).

That does not mean every rough patch is calcium scale, and it does not mean the water itself is gritty. For the full calcium-scale cause list and cleanup path, see [Why Is There Scale on My Hot Tub Shell?](/water-care/why-is-there-scale-on-my-hot-tub-shell).

## Safety note

Use only cleaners approved for your spa shell. Do not scrape acrylic with metal tools or abrasive pads your manual forbids. See [How to Clean a Hot Tub Shell Without Damaging It](/maintenance/how-to-clean-a-hot-tub-shell-without-damaging-it). Test the water before adding chemistry products. Follow product labels. Do not mix different pool or spa chemicals.

## What the texture can mean

### Hard, chalky roughness

If the surface feels hard, chalky, or sandpaper-like, manufacturer language for calcium scale is the closest match ([Sundance Spas](https://www.sundancespas.com/blog-articles/overcoming-hard-water-issues-in-your-hot-tub-3-steps-to-combat-scaling); [Hot Spring Highlife Manual](https://www.hotspring.com/wp-content/uploads/2023/07/highlife-owners-manual-en_us.pdf)). Confirm with a water test for calcium hardness, pH, and alkalinity rather than assuming scale from feel alone. See [Why Is There Scale on My Hot Tub Shell?](/water-care/why-is-there-scale-on-my-hot-tub-shell).

### Soft, slippery, or oily film

Soft brownish film that wipes away with a clean rag is closer to Hot Spring's organic buildup / scum ring path (oils and dirt), not the Scale row ([Hot Spring](https://www.hotspring.com/owners/water-care-and-troubleshooting)). See [Why Is There a Ring Around My Hot Tub Waterline?](/water-care/why-is-there-a-ring-around-my-hot-tub-waterline).

### Colored staining without chalky crust

Green or brown colored marks that remain after wiping may track to Hot Spring's Stains row (iron or copper in source water; low alkalinity and/or pH) rather than calcium scale ([Hot Spring](https://www.hotspring.com/owners/water-care-and-troubleshooting)). See [Why Does My Hot Tub Have Rust-Colored Stains?](/water-care/why-does-my-hot-tub-have-rust-colored-stains).

## Safe checks you can do

1. **Feel and look closely.** Hard chalky crust, soft oily film, or colored stain.
2. **Test calcium hardness, pH, and alkalinity** before adding products. See [How Often Should You Test Hot Tub Water?](/maintenance/how-often-should-you-test-hot-tub-water).
3. **If readings and texture point to scale, follow the scale article** for attributed Hot Spring / Jacuzzi / Sundance guidance rather than inventing a scrub-and-dose plan here.
4. **If you are refilling in a hard-water area, review fill precautions** in [How to Fill a Hot Tub With Hard Water](/water-care/how-to-fill-a-hot-tub-with-hard-water).
5. **Clean only with shell-approved products** if wiping is needed.

## When to stop and get help

Stop scrubbing and stop stacking calcium removers if the roughness remains after labeled chemistry and shell-safe cleaning, if the acrylic is scratched or dull, or if you cannot match readings to your manual. Contact your dealer or a qualified spa technician.
