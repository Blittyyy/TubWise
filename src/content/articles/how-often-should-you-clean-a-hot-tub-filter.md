## Direct answer

There's no single schedule that applies to every hot tub. Jacuzzi, Sundance, and Hot Spring each publish a weekly rinse plus a periodic deep clean, but the exact intervals differ by brand, and even Jacuzzi's own published guides don't fully agree with each other. Your owner's manual is the most reliable source for your specific model.

## Safety note

Turn off power before removing the filter if your owner's manual instructs you to do so, and keep it off for the entire time the filter is out. This is a homeowner-safe task that doesn't require opening the equipment compartment. See [Can You Run a Hot Tub Without a Filter?](/maintenance/can-you-run-a-hot-tub-without-a-filter) if you're wondering whether it's fine to use the spa while the filter is out for cleaning.

## Manufacturer schedules aren't identical, even within one brand

Jacuzzi's filter-cleaning guide recommends a weekly rinse and a deep clean every 4 to 6 weeks, with cartridge replacement every 12 to 24 months or sooner if fibers are torn, flattened, or heavily stained ([Jacuzzi](https://www.jacuzzi.com/en-us/Hot-Tub-Filter-Cleaning-Made-Easy.html)). A separate Jacuzzi troubleshooting guide instead recommends rinsing or backwashing every 4 to 6 weeks and soaking filter elements at least once a month ([Jacuzzi](https://www.jacuzzi.com/en-us/Troubleshooting-Cloudy-Hot-Tub-Water.html)). Both are official Jacuzzi pages, and they describe the deep-clean step differently, which is a useful reminder that even one manufacturer's own materials can vary by document.

Sundance Spas publishes a more specific table: a quick hose rinse every 7 days, a chemical deep clean every 4 to 6 weeks, and full replacement every 12 to 24 months depending on usage ([Sundance Spas](https://www.sundancespas.com/blog-articles/how-to-clean-a-hot-tub-filter-using-sundance-r-spa-filters-the-right-way)).

Watkins Wellness's Hot Spot Collection quick start guide states that filter cartridges should be cleaned monthly, a shorter interval than the 4-to-6-week figure above, but it applies specifically to the Hot Spot Collection (RELAY, RHYTHM, PACE, STRIDE, SX, TX) rather than to Hot Spring spas generally ([Hot Spring Hot Spot Collection guide](https://www.hotspring.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/07/hot-spring-hot-spot-quick-start-guide.pdf)).

## Usage can shorten or extend the interval

Jacuzzi's filter-cleaning guide adjusts its baseline schedule for usage: spas with 4 to 6 bathers several evenings a week, rental or Airbnb use, or frequent sunscreen and cosmetic exposure warrant rinsing every 2 to 3 days and deep cleaning every 3 to 4 weeks, while winter-only use with 1 to 2 people can stretch to rinsing every 2 weeks and deep cleaning every 6 to 8 weeks ([Jacuzzi](https://www.jacuzzi.com/en-us/Hot-Tub-Filter-Cleaning-Made-Easy.html)). Sundance makes a similar point for rental properties, vacation homes, and spas near dusty roads or trees, suggesting a rinse every few days and a deep clean every 2 to 3 weeks in those situations ([Sundance Spas](https://www.sundancespas.com/blog-articles/how-to-clean-a-hot-tub-filter-using-sundance-r-spa-filters-the-right-way)). See [Why Does My Hot Tub Filter Get Dirty So Fast?](/maintenance/why-does-my-hot-tub-filter-get-dirty-so-fast) if your filter seems to need attention more often than any of these baselines suggest.

The pattern across both manufacturers is the same even though the exact numbers differ: both frame the baseline schedule as adjustable, not fixed, and both tie the adjustment to specific, observable conditions (bather count, rental use, cosmetics exposure, or the opposite: light, seasonal use). Treat the numbers above as a reasonable range to test against your own filter's condition and your own usage pattern, rather than an exact countdown that applies regardless of how the spa is actually used.

## Why the interval isn't universal

It's worth being direct about why this article doesn't give a single number: the manufacturer guidance itself doesn't converge on one. Two official Jacuzzi pages describe two different deep-clean cadences, and a Hot Spring document for one specific spa collection describes a third. None of these guides is wrong; they're simply describing different models, different documents, or different levels of general versus specific guidance. That's exactly why checking your own owner's manual, rather than any single brand example (including the ones cited here), is the most reliable way to set your actual schedule.

## Safe checks the homeowner can do

1. **Find your model's own schedule first.** Check your owner's manual for a filter-cleaning interval before defaulting to any brand example above.
2. **Set a weekly rinse reminder** regardless of brand, since every manufacturer reviewed here recommends at least that baseline.
3. **Watch your actual usage**, not just the calendar. More bathers, sunscreen, or rental use are documented reasons to clean more often.
4. **Inspect the filter each time you rinse it** for damage, discoloration, or flattened pleats, since that can mean replacement is due sooner than any interval. See [When Should You Replace a Hot Tub Filter?](/maintenance/when-should-you-replace-a-hot-tub-filter) for what to look for.
5. **Keep a simple log** of rinse and deep-clean dates so you can tell whether your actual schedule matches what your manual recommends.

## When to stop and call a technician

- The water stays cloudy or the filter looks dirty again within a day or two of cleaning, even on a reasonable schedule
- Cleaning on schedule doesn't resolve weak jets or heating issues. See [Can a Dirty Filter Make Hot Tub Water Cloudy?](/water-care/can-a-dirty-filter-make-hot-tub-water-cloudy) and [Can a Dirty Filter Stop a Hot Tub From Heating?](/troubleshooting/can-a-dirty-filter-stop-a-hot-tub-from-heating) if either symptom is also present
- You're not sure a full cleaning cycle is actually removing buildup. See [How to Clean a Hot Tub Filter](/maintenance/how-to-clean-a-hot-tub-filter) for the method itself

None of these require guessing. If a reasonable cleaning schedule isn't keeping the water clear or the flow steady, the filter itself, or something else in the system, may need a closer look than routine cleaning can provide.
