## Direct answer

Noise that shows up mainly when the jets are on is not automatically a failed pump. On Hot Spot Collection spas, air-control valves deliberately add air to the jets, and Watkins notes that turning the air control closed when the spa is not in use helps the spa operate more quietly (Hot Spot Collection owner's manual). Jacuzzi treats loud or unusual sounds from circulating pumps as a red flag and links some growling or abnormal pump noise to low water or trapped air (Jacuzzi). Separate expected air-and-water jet sound from a new harsh, grinding, or growling equipment sound before you assume the motor has failed.

This page is about timing (jets on). It is not a full sound catalog. For growling, squealing, or humming patterns in general, see [Why Is My Hot Tub Pump Making Noise?](/troubleshooting/why-is-my-hot-tub-pump-making-noise).

## Safety note

Do not open the control box. Do not touch pumps or fittings while they are running. Shut off power at the breaker before any equipment-bay inspection your manual allows (Jacuzzi). Do not run a pump dry. Do not keep operating equipment that is grinding, smoking, overheating, or leaking near electrical parts. Call a qualified spa technician when the next step would be motor or electrical work.

## Expected jet sound versus a warning sound

### Air mixed into the jets

Hot Spot Collection manuals describe an air-control valve that sends air volume to the jets, and they tell owners to close that control when the spa is idle so the spa runs more quietly with the cover on (Hot Spot Collection owner's manual). If the louder sound tracks with the air control being open and softens when you close it from the topside controls, that pattern fits designed air induction on those spas. Other brands use different air controls; confirm the labels in your own manual.

That expected rush or hiss is different from a deep growling pump sound or a high-pitched mechanical squeal.

### Growling or abnormal pump noise while jets run

Jacuzzi states that low water can cause a deep growling sound and that raising the water may resolve that issue (Jacuzzi). Jacuzzi also ties growling or abnormal pump noise to trapped air that should be bled using the manufacturer's method (Jacuzzi). Jacuzzi warns that loud and unusual sounds from circulating pumps mean something is wrong with the pump or the water lines (Jacuzzi).

Do not label every jet-time noise as cavitation. If flow is weak or surging while the pump sounds abnormal, treat that as a flow problem first and use the linked guides below rather than naming a pump failure from sound alone.

### Vibration versus jet-time noise

If the main complaint is felt shaking through the cabinet or pad rather than sound alone, see [Why Is My Hot Tub Vibrating?](/troubleshooting/why-is-my-hot-tub-vibrating).

## Safe checks that stay topside

1. **Confirm whether the sound appears only with jets on**, or also during quiet filtration.
2. **Change the air control from the topside** if your spa has one. On Hot Spot Collection models, closing air when idle is documented for quieter operation (Hot Spot Collection owner's manual). Note whether the sound follows the air setting.
3. **Check water level against your manual.** Jacuzzi links low water to air in suction lines and growling (Jacuzzi). Hot Spot manuals also tie weak or surging jets after a fill check to low water or clogged filters (Hot Spot Collection owner's manual).
4. **Inspect and clean the filter** if it is dirty or collapsed. Jacuzzi and Hot Spot both connect restricted filters to poor jet performance (Jacuzzi; Hot Spot Collection owner's manual). See [How to Clean a Hot Tub Filter](/maintenance/how-to-clean-a-hot-tub-filter).
5. **Compare jet strength.** Weak or surging flow has its own check order in [Why Are My Hot Tub Jets Weak?](/troubleshooting/why-are-my-hot-tub-jets-weak). After a refill, also see [How to Tell if a Hot Tub Has an Air Lock](/troubleshooting/how-to-tell-if-a-hot-tub-has-an-air-lock).
6. **Do not loosen unions, open the pack, or spray lubricants** to chase jet-time noise.

## When to stop and call a technician

- Harsh growling, grinding, or screaming continues after water level and filter checks
- Noise continues after your manual's air-purge steps following a refill
- Jets stay weak while the pump sounds abnormal
- You smell burning, see heat damage, or find water on equipment
- Any repair would require opening the motor, wet end, or control box

Jacuzzi directs electrical work and pump replacement to a qualified spa technician (Jacuzzi).
