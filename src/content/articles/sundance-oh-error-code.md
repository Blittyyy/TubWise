## Exact scoped meaning

In the cited **Sundance 680 Series** owner's manual Error Conditions section (**Panel Displays OH**), the message means water temperature is **above acceptable limits**. That entry warns of hyperthermia risk and states:

**DO NOT ENTER SPA!**

In that same 680 Series OH entry, water temperature has reached **112°F (44°C)** and the **low speed pump** has activated to circulate water through the heater.

This is the Sundance **OH** definition for that 680 Series manual text only. It is **not** Jacuzzi OH, Gecko OH, or a Watchdog dash display. Do not treat **112°F (44°C)** as a universal hot-tub overheat limit.

## Scope warning

Use this article only if your spa is covered by the cited **Sundance 680 Series** manual and your panel shows **OH** with matching overheat wording. Other Sundance series or newer manuals may use different overheat messages or different thresholds.

Confirm the exact OH (or overheat) text in **your** Sundance owner's manual before acting. If your manual does not list OH this way, follow your manual instead of this page.

## What the code can indicate

In the cited 680 Series OH entry, the control system is reporting that spa water is **too hot** relative to that manual’s limit. It is a safety condition, not a diagnosis of one failed part.

Do not treat OH as proof of a bad sensor, bad heater, or bad board. Sensor and board diagnosis is technician work.

For general heating behavior after the spa is safe again, see [Hot Tub Not Heating: What to Check First](/troubleshooting/hot-tub-not-heating). Refill situations are separate: [Hot Tub Not Heating After a Refill](/troubleshooting/hot-tub-not-heating-after-a-refill).

## Safe owner-level checks

**First: do not enter the water** while OH is displayed, as the cited 680 Series manual requires.

Then:

1. **Keep people and pets out of the spa** until the overheat message is cleared and the water is at a safe temperature per your manual.
2. **Allow the spa to cool as that OH entry describes.** In the cited 680 Series OH text, the low speed pump is already activated to circulate water through the heater. That entry does **not** list cover removal, ice, chemicals, or a specific resume temperature. Do not add cooling methods your manual does not print.
3. **Do not keep using the spa** while OH is active.
4. **Contact an authorized Sundance dealer** if the condition persists or if your manual directs dealer service for ongoing overheat.

If a different Sundance manual you own lists additional owner cooling steps for OH, follow **that** manual’s wording only for that spa. Do not import those steps into a 680 Series spa whose OH entry does not include them.

## What not to do

Do not enter the spa. Do not add ice, cold water, or chemicals to force cooling. Do not bypass a high-limit device. Do not open the control box. Do not measure sensors with a meter. Do not apply Jacuzzi OH or Gecko OH temperature numbers to a Sundance 680 Series spa. Do not repeatedly reset the breaker to clear OH. Do not assume one part failed from the code alone.

## When to stop and call a technician

Contact an authorized Sundance dealer or qualified technician if:

- OH returns after the water has cooled, or the condition persists
- the panel shows Watchdog dashes or another overheat shutdown that deactivates the spa (see [Hot Tub Control Panel Is Flashing or Showing Dashes](/troubleshooting/hot-tub-control-panel-is-flashing-or-showing-dashes) for how that differs from OH)
- a GFCI or breaker trips, will not reset, or trips again after one reset
- you smell burning, see scorching, damaged wiring, or leaks near electrical equipment
- diagnosis would require sensor testing or opening an electrical enclosure
- you cannot confirm your manual’s OH scope or thresholds

## How to confirm the code in the owner's manual

1. Identify your Sundance series and model.
2. Open Error Conditions / Error Messages for **Panel Displays OH**.
3. Match **OH** on the panel to that entry, including any **DO NOT ENTER** wording and temperature figures.
4. Follow only the owner actions listed for your manual revision.
5. Treat Jacuzzi, Gecko, and Balboa overheat pages as different products with different limits.
