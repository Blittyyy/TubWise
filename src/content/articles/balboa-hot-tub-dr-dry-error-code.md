## Direct answer

On documented Balboa systems, **Heater may be dry** and **Heater is dry** mean the control believes there is not enough water in the heater path to start or continue heating safely (Balboa Spa Touch 3 / TP7 message guides). On some legacy and simplified-menu panels, the same family appears as short codes such as **dr** (heater may be dry) and **drY** / **Heater is Dry** (Balboa TP500 simplified-menu guide).

Those messages are protective warnings about heater water / flow conditions. They are **not** proof by themselves that the heating element has failed.

## Safety note

Do not repeatedly force resets if the warning returns. Do not run the spa without a filter when your manufacturer requires one. Do not dry-run pumps. Do not open the control box, test electrical parts, disassemble the heater, or bypass temperature or flow sensors. If dry-heater or related flow messages persist after safe owner checks, shut the system down as your manual directs and call a qualified spa technician.

## Initial dry warning vs persistent dry condition

Balboa’s Spa Touch 3 guide distinguishes the two heater-dry messages this way:

| Message (Spa Touch 3 wording) | Code note in that guide | Documented behavior |
| --- | --- | --- |
| The heater may be dry | M028 | Possible dry heater, or not enough water in the heater to start it. Spa shut down for about **15 minutes**. Reset the message to retry heater start-up after checks. |
| The heater is dry | M027 | Not enough water in the heater to start it. Spa shut down. After fixing the condition, reset the message to restart heater start-up. |

TP7 uses the same descriptive pair: an initial “may be dry” condition that retries on a delay, and a stronger “heater is dry” condition that requires clearing the message after the problem is resolved (Balboa).

The TP500 simplified-menu guide documents the same pair with panel text **Heater may be Dry (dr)** and **Heater is Dry**, including the about-15-minute pause on the “may be dry” message (Balboa).

Do **not** assume a universal number of occurrences, retry interval, or reset sequence across every Balboa pack. Use the reset and timing language in **your** panel guide only.

## A full spa can still have a dry-heater warning

Dry-heater protection watches conditions at the **heater**, not only whether the shell looks full. Balboa’s flow-related checks list low water level, suction restrictions, closed valves, trapped air, too many closed jets, and pump priming (Balboa Spa Touch 3). Restricted filters and post-refill air can reduce heater flow even when the waterline looks normal. Related guides: [Can a Dirty Filter Stop a Hot Tub From Heating?](/troubleshooting/can-a-dirty-filter-stop-a-hot-tub-from-heating), [How to Tell if a Hot Tub Has an Air Lock](/troubleshooting/how-to-tell-if-a-hot-tub-has-an-air-lock), and [Hot Tub Not Heating After a Refill](/troubleshooting/hot-tub-not-heating-after-a-refill).

## Observation-based troubleshooting table

| What you notice | More consistent with | Owner-safe next step |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Message after a refill or power cycle | Air still in plumbing / incomplete prime | Confirm Priming Mode completed successfully; see [What Does Pr Mean on a Balboa Hot Tub?](/error-codes/balboa/pr) |
| Waterline below skimmer / suction | Low water level | Refill to the manufacturer waterline, then follow your panel’s reset instructions if provided |
| Filter overdue for cleaning | Restricted flow | Clean or replace the filter per your spa brand’s instructions |
| Jets weak, surging, or noisy pump | Possible trapped air or suction issue | Use only owner priming steps in your manual; stop if flow will not establish |
| Many jets closed on a jet-heavy spa | Reduced flow path | Open jets as your manual allows for heating/circulation |
| Warning clears once and does not return | Temporary start-up flow issue | Monitor through a heat cycle; keep the cover on while heating unless your chemical label requires otherwise |
| Warning returns immediately after every reset | Persistent heater-flow or equipment fault | Stop repeated resets; call qualified service |
| Burning smell, scorched parts, or GFCI trips | Electrical / equipment hazard | Power off and call a technician |

## What to record before contacting service

Write down:

- Exact panel text (and any code such as M027 / M028 if shown)
- Whether the spa was recently drained or refilled
- Water level relative to the manufacturer mark
- Whether Priming Mode / **Pr** completed with visible jet flow
- Filter condition and last cleaning date
- Whether jets or valves were closed
- How many times the message returned after a single careful reset attempt allowed by your manual

That information helps a technician without encouraging unsafe testing.

## What these codes do not prove

A dry-heater message means the control interrupted heater start-up because it did not detect adequate heater water/flow conditions. It does **not** by itself identify a failed element, a bad pack, or a bad sensor. Balboa’s owner-facing flow checks come first; deeper diagnosis belongs to trained service using manufacturer procedures.
