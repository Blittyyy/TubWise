## Direct answer

Surging or pulsating jets are about flow that keeps changing, not about a single steady weak stream. Hot Spring's troubleshooting chart groups "jet operation is weak or surging" and lists low water, clogged filters, and a closed Comfort Control lever as causes to check (Hot Spring). Hot Spot Collection manuals say weak or surging jets during the post-fill jet check can mean low water or clogged filters (Hot Spot Collection owner's manual). Hot Spot quick-start guidance treats strong, non-surging flow as the sign that priming is complete on those spas (Hot Spot Collection quick start guide). Surging does not, by itself, prove cavitation, a blocked impeller, or a failed pump.

If the jets are simply weak but steady, start with [Why Are My Hot Tub Jets Weak?](/troubleshooting/why-are-my-hot-tub-jets-weak). This page stays on rhythmic or on-off pulsing.

## Safety note

Follow your manual for filter and topside valve access. Do not open the control box. Do not tear down the pump. Do not run a pump dry (Jacuzzi). If surging comes with grinding, overheating, leaks near electrical parts, breaker trips, or a burning smell, shut power off and call a qualified spa technician.

## What "surging" means here

Surging means the jet push rises and falls, sputters, or pulses instead of staying even. Hot Spot Collection quick-start material uses non-surging flow as a priming checkpoint and tells owners on those spas to shut a surging jet off for five seconds and turn it back on while checking diverters (Hot Spot Collection quick start guide). That is Hot Spot Collection scope only. Other brands may document a different purge method.

Hot Spot Collection manuals also note that air-control levers change air volume to the jets (Hot Spot Collection owner's manual). Changing air can change how the jet feels or sounds. That is not the same as a pump failure.

## Causes manufacturers document for this pattern

### Water level too low

Hot Spring lists low spa water as a cause of weak or surging jet operation, with adding water as the fix (Hot Spring). Jacuzzi notes that low water lets air into suction lines and weakens flow (Jacuzzi). Check your manual's fill line first.

### Clogged filter or suction restriction

Hot Spring lists clogged filters for the same weak-or-surging chart entry (Hot Spring). Hot Spot manuals repeat low water or clogged filters when jets are weak or surging after a fill check (Hot Spot Collection owner's manual). See [How to Clean a Hot Tub Filter](/maintenance/how-to-clean-a-hot-tub-filter).

### Closed jet, diverter, or air-related topside controls

Hot Spring lists a closed Comfort Control lever for its own spas on that chart entry (Hot Spring). Hot Spot manuals tell owners to open the air lever and open jet faces that can be shut off before judging the jet check (Hot Spot Collection owner's manual). Confirm names and positions in your manual.

### Trapped air after a drain and refill

Jacuzzi lists airlock after refilling among common pump problems (Jacuzzi). If surging started right after a refill, read [How to Tell if a Hot Tub Has an Air Lock](/troubleshooting/how-to-tell-if-a-hot-tub-has-an-air-lock) and [Why Is My Hot Tub Making Noise After Filling or Refilling?](/troubleshooting/why-is-my-hot-tub-making-noise-after-filling-or-refilling). Follow only your manufacturer's purge steps.

### Pump speed changes

Jacuzzi describes most spa pumps as two-speed units, with low speed used for filtration and heating cycles (Jacuzzi). A change in feel when the spa switches modes can be normal control behavior on those systems. Compare the same jet mode before calling the flow a fault.

## Safe checks in order

1. **Match the waterline** to your manual and add water if it is low (Hot Spring; Jacuzzi).
2. **Inspect and clean the filter** (Hot Spring; Hot Spot Collection owner's manual).
3. **Open topside jet, diverter, and air controls** your manual uses for a full jet test (Hot Spring; Hot Spot Collection owner's manual).
4. **Note whether the surge affects all jets or one group**, and whether it started after a refill.
5. **Follow your manual's purge or priming checkpoint** if the timing fits a refill. On Hot Spot Collection quick-start steps, strong non-surging flow is the clear target (Hot Spot Collection quick start guide).
6. **Stop continuous operation** if surging is paired with grinding, burning smells, leaks, or breaker trips.

## When to stop and call a technician

- Surging continues after water level, filter, and topside valve checks
- Manual purge steps do not produce steady flow
- Surging returns after every refill
- You also hear grinding, humming without flow, or other abnormal pump noise; see [Why Is My Hot Tub Pump Making Noise?](/troubleshooting/why-is-my-hot-tub-pump-making-noise)
- The spa also will not heat after flow checks; see [Hot Tub Has Power but No Heat](/troubleshooting/hot-tub-has-power-but-no-heat)

Anything beyond topside water, filter, and documented purge steps is a fair reason to call for service.
