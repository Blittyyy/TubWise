## Direct answer

Start with power, the GFCI, water level, filter condition, and any panel warning. Low flow from a dirty filter or air lock can stop the heater, while repeated breaker trips or sensor faults require qualified service.

## Safety warning

Do not open the control box. Do not touch wiring. Do not bypass a GFCI, breaker, high-limit switch, flow switch, or pressure switch.

**Reset once only if permitted by the owner's manual. If it will not reset or trips again, stop and contact qualified service.**

If a GFCI will not test or reset correctly, Hot Spring warns this can signal an electrical malfunction and shock risk. Leave power off until a qualified technician corrects the fault.

## No power vs. power but no heat

**No power:** The panel is dark. Lights, jets, and heat do not respond.

**Power but no heat:** The panel is on, or lights and jets still work, but the water does not warm.

Hot Spring treats these as different cases. An inoperative spa may have a power failure, tripped GFCI, tripped high-limit thermostat, or spa lock. A spa that will not heat while jets and lights still run may have an open pressure switch, circulation-pump thermal cut-off, or air lock after filling (Hot Spring). See [Hot Tub Has Power but No Heat](/troubleshooting/hot-tub-has-power-but-no-heat) for the detailed check order for that specific case.

## Common things to check

### No power or tripped GFCI

If nothing works, check the spa's power source and GFCI or breaker first (Hot Spring; Jacuzzi).

### Spa lock or economy mode

Hot Spring lists an activated spa lock as a cause of an inoperative spa. A legacy Jacuzzi manual for certain older models lists an economy filtration mode that does not control water temperature continuously. Check your own manual for any similar setting (Hot Spring; Jacuzzi).

### Wrong set temperature

Confirm the set temperature on the panel. On some Hot Spring Highlife models, a ready indicator lights only when the water is within about 2°F of the set temperature (Hot Spring Highlife owner's manual).

### Low water

Low water can reduce flow and stop heating. Use the fill mark in your owner's manual (Hot Spring; Jacuzzi).

### Dirty filter

A clogged filter can reduce flow, trip safety cutoffs, or deactivate the heater. Inspect or clean it only by following your manufacturer's directions (Hot Spring; Jacuzzi). See [Can a Dirty Filter Stop a Hot Tub From Heating?](/troubleshooting/can-a-dirty-filter-stop-a-hot-tub-from-heating) for a closer look at how this happens, or [How to Clean a Hot Tub Filter](/maintenance/how-to-clean-a-hot-tub-filter) for the cleaning method itself.

### Air lock after refill

If the problem started after a refill, trapped air can stop proper flow and heating. Follow your manual's air-lock steps only (Hot Spring; Jacuzzi). See [Hot Tub Not Heating After a Refill](/troubleshooting/hot-tub-not-heating-after-a-refill) for the manufacturer-documented purge steps, or [How to Tell if a Hot Tub Has an Air Lock](/troubleshooting/how-to-tell-if-a-hot-tub-has-an-air-lock) if you're not sure that's what's happening.

### High-limit or flow safety trip

Spas shut the heater off when flow or temperature protection is triggered. Hot Spring documents a blinking power indicator as a high-limit trip: water too hot, or low circulation such as from clogged filters (Hot Spring Highlife owner's manual). Some older Jacuzzi models use FL1 or FL2 for different flow-switch faults that disable the heater; sensor codes such as SN1 or SN2 require dealer repair (Jacuzzi).

Do not assume the heater has failed. These symptoms alone are not enough to diagnose the heating element.

## Safe checks the homeowner can do

Work through these in order. Stop if any step tells you to. Do not open the control box or touch wiring.

1. **Check for power.** See whether the panel lights up and whether jets or lights respond.
2. **Check the set temperature.** Raise it only if it is set lower than you expect.
3. **Look for a spa lock or economy setting.** Follow your manual if either is listed for your spa.
4. **Write down any error code or blinking indicator.** Look up the meaning in your owner's manual.
5. **Check the water level against your manual.** Add water only to that mark.
6. **Watch the jets.** Weak or surging flow can mean low water or a dirty filter (Hot Spring). Observe only—do not open the pump. See [Why Are My Hot Tub Jets Weak?](/troubleshooting/why-are-my-hot-tub-jets-weak) if jets stay weak on their own.
7. **Turn power off, then inspect or clean the filter.** Jacuzzi directs owners to shut off power at the main breaker and spa disconnect before inspecting components, then follow your manual's filter steps (Jacuzzi; Hot Spring).
8. **If you recently refilled the spa, follow your manual's air-lock instructions.**
9. **If the spa has no power, check the GFCI or breaker.** Reset once only if permitted by the owner's manual. If it will not reset or trips again, stop and contact qualified service.

## When to stop and call a technician

Stop and call qualified service if:

- The GFCI or breaker will not reset, or trips again after one attempt
- A high-limit, heater, or circulation-pump protective trip keeps returning
- Flow does not return after your manual's filter, water-level, and air-lock steps
- The panel shows a sensor or flow-switch fault that your manual says needs dealer repair
- You would need to open the control box, test wiring, or bypass a safety device
- The spa still will not heat after the safe checks above
- You notice a burning smell, scorched parts, or another electrical warning sign
- You suspect the heating element or another internal component has failed
