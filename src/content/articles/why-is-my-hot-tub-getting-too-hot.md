## Direct answer

If the spa feels hotter than the setting, shows an overheat message, or trips high-limit protection, stop and verify before soaking. Hot Spring states that spa water should never exceed 104°F and summarizes Consumer Product Safety Commission guidance that temperatures above 104°F are dangerous for adults (Hot Spring). Brand overheat codes such as Jacuzzi or Sundance OH are separate, manual-scoped events. Confirm the exact code and wording in your own manual. See [Jacuzzi OH Error Code](/error-codes/jacuzzi/oh) or [Sundance OH Error Code](/error-codes/sundance/oh) when those brands apply.

## Safety note

Do not enter water you believe is overheating until you have confirmed it is safe by your manufacturer's instructions. Do not open the control box. Do not defeat, jumper, or bypass a high-limit switch, temperature sensor, flow or pressure switch, thermostat, or GFCI. Do not keep resetting protective devices that will not hold. If the spa is overheating, tripping breakers, producing burning smells, showing melted components, or behaving unpredictably, shut off power and contact a qualified spa technician.

## Too hot vs other temperature problems

**Getting too hot:** Water exceeds the expected set temperature, feels scalding relative to the display, or the spa signals overheat / high-limit.

**Not reaching set temperature:** Water stays cooler than the selected set point. See [Why Is My Hot Tub Not Reaching the Set Temperature?](/troubleshooting/why-is-my-hot-tub-not-reaching-the-set-temperature).

**Temperature keeps dropping:** Water was warm and then falls. See [Why Does My Hot Tub Temperature Keep Dropping?](/troubleshooting/why-does-my-hot-tub-temperature-keep-dropping).

## Verify the reading before you assume a runaway heater

### Compare set point, display, and how the water feels

On certain Hot Spring Highlife models, a Ready indicator means water is within about 2°F of the set temperature (Hot Spring Highlife owner's manual). Feeling hotter than expected can mean the set point is higher than you remember, a guest changed it, or the displayed value does not match an independent spa-water measurement.

Hot Spring instructs users to measure water temperature with an accurate thermometer before entering (Hot Spring Highlife owner's manual). Use a thermometer suitable for spa-water use. Do not rely on an ordinary household thermometer that is not rated for that duty.

### Manufacturer temperature limits are not universal

Hot Spring's Highlife safety section states water should never exceed 104°F, with 100°F to 104°F described as the range considered safe for a healthy adult in that manual, and a 100°F limit noted for pregnant or possibly pregnant women (Hot Spring Highlife owner's manual). Do not treat those figures as every brand's control-panel maximum.

Jacuzzi and Sundance OH pages on TubWise summarize manufacturer documentation in which OH can appear around 112°F for those specific systems, with do-not-enter instructions. That threshold is overheat-code behavior for those manuals, not a recommended soak setting and not a universal spa limit.

## Causes owners can check without opening equipment

### Wrong or raised set temperature

Confirm the set temperature on the panel. Hot Spring recommends keeping adult settings at or below 104°F (Hot Spring). Do not raise the set point above your manufacturer's limit.

### Cover left on while intentionally cooling

Hot Spring notes that its spas retain heat when covered, so owners who want cooler water should leave the cover open while temperature drops (Hot Spring). A closed cover is normal for holding heat; it works against intentional cooling.

### High-limit trip from overheating or low circulation

Hot Spring documents a flashing Power / high-limit condition as water above an acceptable temperature or low circulation such as clogged filters, with a power disconnect of about 30 seconds to reset and service if trips continue (Hot Spring). Filter and flow checks belong in manufacturer cleaning steps only. See [Can a Dirty Filter Stop a Hot Tub From Heating?](/troubleshooting/can-a-dirty-filter-stop-a-hot-tub-from-heating) and [Why Does My Hot Tub Keep Turning Off?](/troubleshooting/why-does-my-hot-tub-keep-turning-off).

### Brand overheat error codes

If the panel shows OH or another overheat code, identify the exact brand and controller documentation. Do not treat one brand's OH definition as universal. Use the brand-scoped TubWise pages linked above, then confirm against your printed or PDF manual.

## What not to do

- Do not continue soaking through an overheat warning
- Do not bypass high-limit or sensor protection
- Do not keep power-cycling after repeated high-limit trips
- Do not assume solar gain, summer weather, or a "stuck heater" without manufacturer-supported evidence from your own spa's indicators and manual

## When to call a technician

Call qualified service if high-limit or overheat conditions return after one manual-directed reset and filter check, if the displayed temperature and a spa-rated thermometer disagree in a way you cannot explain from set-point error, or if electrical smell, smoke, melted parts, or breaker trips accompany the heat problem.

## Bottom line

"Too hot" is a safety stop first and a diagnosis second. Stay within manufacturer temperature limits, confirm the reading with spa-appropriate tools when your manual says to, and treat repeating high-limit or OH events as technician work, never as a homeowner bypass project.
