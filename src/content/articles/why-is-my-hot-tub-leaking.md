## Direct answer

A dropping water level is not automatically a plumbing failure. Jacuzzi describes leaks from the pump or heater and natural evaporation as separate reasons water levels change (Jacuzzi). Look for wet spots or pooling near plumbing connections, turn power off if you suspect a leak, and treat anything beyond a simple, visible check as technician work.

## Safety note

If you suspect a leak, Jacuzzi advises turning off the power supply to help limit further damage (Jacuzzi). Do not open the control box. Do not touch wiring. Do not run the spa with the equipment compartment door removed if your manufacturer forbids it; Hot Spot Collection manuals warn against that practice (Hot Spring Hot Spot Collection guide). Water near electrical equipment is a stop condition for homeowner troubleshooting.

## Evaporation is not the same as a leak

Jacuzzi lists natural evaporation alongside true leaks when explaining water-level fluctuations (Jacuzzi). Hot Spot Collection water-care guidance also notes that water evaporates from a spa and that keeping the cover in place helps minimize evaporation (Hot Spring Hot Spot Collection guide).

Before assuming fittings have failed:

- Confirm whether the cover was left open or poorly seated overnight
- Note weather that can increase surface loss, such as wind or heat, without treating weather alone as proof of a leak
- Compare slow, even drop with no wet ground against visible wet spots near plumbing

For overnight drop specifically, see [Hot Tub Losing Water Overnight: Leak or Evaporation?](/troubleshooting/hot-tub-losing-water-overnight-leak-or-evaporation).

## Where homeowners can look safely

Jacuzzi tells owners to inspect for wet spots or water pooling around the spa, particularly near plumbing connections (Jacuzzi). Jacuzzi's repair basics page also links worn seals around jets and plumbing connections to leaks that can worsen over time, and notes that leaky seals can create significant water loss and potential electrical hazards if left unchecked (Jacuzzi).

Safe visual checks only:

1. **Power off** at the spa disconnect or breaker before opening any equipment access your manual allows.
2. **Walk the cabinet perimeter** for damp ground, stains, or fresh wet spots.
3. **Look near accessible plumbing connections** without loosening pressurized fittings unless your own manual documents that exact step.
4. **Note whether water appears only after jets run.** That pattern belongs to [Why Does My Hot Tub Lose Water When the Jets Are On?](/troubleshooting/why-does-my-hot-tub-lose-water-when-the-jets-are-on), not to a general static inspection.

If water is collecting under the spa or inside the equipment bay, use the focused checks in [Hot Tub Leaking From Underneath: What to Check](/troubleshooting/hot-tub-leaking-from-underneath).

## What this article does not diagnose

A wet spot does not identify which part failed. Jacuzzi mentions pump and heater leaks as possible sources; it does not authorize owners to replace seals, crack shells, dig into foam, or pressure-test plumbing as DIY steps (Jacuzzi). Hot Spot Collection manuals document hand-tight union checks in the equipment compartment for that product line; that is not a universal repair for every brand (Hot Spring Hot Spot Collection guide).

Do not:

- apply sealants or epoxy based on internet repair kits
- dig into foam insulation to chase a leak
- open the control pack
- keep resetting breakers if water is near electrical gear

## Safe checks the homeowner can do

1. **Mark the waterline** with tape or a photo so you can tell whether the level is still falling.
2. **Confirm the cover fits and stays closed** when the spa is idle.
3. **Top off only to your manual's fill line.** Jacuzzi describes keeping water just above the skimmer intake as part of stable operation (Jacuzzi).
4. **Power off, then look for wet spots** near plumbing and the cabinet base.
5. **Stop and call service** if you see water on or near electrical components, cannot find the source, or the level keeps dropping after evaporation and cover issues are ruled out.

## When to stop and call a technician

- Water is near the control pack, heater terminals, or other electrical parts
- The spa loses water quickly with the cover closed and no obvious splash path
- You would need to open pressurized plumbing, replace seals, or cut into the shell or foam
- The leak returns after a dealer-documented union check on a model that allows that step

Persistent leaks and electrical hazards are service issues, not filter or chemistry problems.
