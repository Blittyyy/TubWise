## Direct answer

Water under the spa or inside the cabinet often points to something in the equipment area being wet, not to the acrylic shell as the first place to cut open. For Hot Spot Collection spas, Watkins documents that equipment-compartment unions can loosen and leak if they are not hand-tight (Hot Spring Hot Spot Collection guide). Jacuzzi separately names pump and heater leaks among causes of water-level problems and tells owners to power off when a leak is suspected (Jacuzzi).

## Safety note

Turn power off at the breaker or disconnect before opening the equipment access panel (Jacuzzi). Do not open the control box. Do not touch wiring, heater terminals, or wet electrical parts. Hot Spot Collection manuals say not to use the spa with the equipment compartment door removed (Hot Spring Hot Spot Collection guide). If water is on electrical components, stop and call a technician.

## Why "underneath" is a location clue, not a diagnosis

Water runs downhill. A drip from a pump union, heater connection, or wet-end seal can pool at the cabinet floor or soak the pad under the spa. Jacuzzi lists pump or heater leaks among leak sources and points owners toward wet spots near plumbing connections (Jacuzzi). That does not prove which fitting failed from a pad stain alone.

This page stays in the equipment-bay zone. For a wider evaporation-versus-leak triage, see [Why Is My Hot Tub Leaking?](/troubleshooting/why-is-my-hot-tub-leaking).

## Manufacturer-documented union checks (Hot Spot Collection)

On Hot Spot Collection spas, Watkins states that union fittings in the equipment compartment may loosen during transportation and that owners should check they are hand-tight before filling to avoid leaks (Hot Spring Hot Spot Collection guide).

The same manual's priming steps describe loosening the union on top of the pump to release air, then hand-tightening it, restoring power, activating the pump, and confirming the union is tight enough to keep it from leaking before reinstalling the equipment door (Hot Spring Hot Spot Collection guide).

Scope limits:

- That union guidance applies to the cited Hot Spot Collection manual, not every spa brand.
- Hand-tight means follow the manual's wording. Do not force fittings with large wrenches unless your manual says to.
- Priming and union steps that require equipment-bay access are only for owners comfortable with the exact procedure in their book. If you are not, call a dealer.

## Pump-area wetness Jacuzzi describes

Jacuzzi's circulation guide states that leaks under the pump can signal pump-seal failure (Jacuzzi). Jacuzzi's pump guide separately says water around the pump can indicate a failed shaft seal or cracked housing and that those conditions require professional repair (Jacuzzi). Treat that as a reason to stop homeowner repair attempts, not as a parts-replacement tutorial.

## Safe checks the homeowner can do

1. **Shut off power** and verify the topside is dark before opening equipment access.
2. **Look without touching electrical parts** for active drips, wet fittings, or mineral trails on pump and heater plumbing.
3. **If your spa is a Hot Spot Collection model and your manual matches**, confirm equipment-compartment unions are hand-tight as that book describes.
4. **Dry the area, restore power only if the bay is dry and your manual allows it**, then briefly run jets while watching for a fresh drip at unions. Power off again if water appears near electrical gear. See [Why Does My Hot Tub Lose Water When the Jets Are On?](/troubleshooting/why-does-my-hot-tub-lose-water-when-the-jets-are-on) for the pressurized-leak pattern.
5. **Leave the equipment door secured** when finished if your manufacturer requires it.

## When to stop and call a technician

- Water is on or under the control pack, heater electrical connections, or pump motor end
- You see wetness at the pump wet end that Jacuzzi-style guidance treats as a seal or housing issue
- Unions keep weeping after a documented hand-tight check
- Finding the source would mean cutting foam, digging under the shell, or replacing seals
- The spa also makes grinding or loud bearing-type noise; see [Why Is My Hot Tub Pump Making Noise?](/troubleshooting/why-is-my-hot-tub-pump-making-noise)

Under-spa water plus electrical risk is not a filter or chemistry fix.
