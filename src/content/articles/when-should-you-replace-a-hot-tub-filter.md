## Direct answer

Replace a filter when it shows physical damage, or when the water stays cloudy or flow stays weak despite balanced chemistry and a clean filter, regardless of age. Age-based replacement intervals are documented too, but they vary widely, and Jacuzzi's own setup guide even gives two different intervals (quarterly and annual) within the same document without reconciling them. Treat any specific number as a starting point rather than a rule, and rely on your owner's manual and the physical signs below as the deciding factors.

## Safety note

Turn off power before removing the old filter if your owner's manual instructs you to do so. Replacing a filter is a homeowner task that doesn't require tools beyond what came with the cartridge.

## Signs it's time to replace, not just clean

Jacuzzi's filter-care guide lists specific signs that a filter needs replacing rather than another cleaning cycle: persistent cloudiness despite balanced water chemistry, limp or flattened pleats that no longer spring back, a strong odor that survives thorough cleaning, visible cracks in the end caps, and recurring flow or FLO errors immediately after cleaning ([Jacuzzi](https://www.jacuzzi.com/en-us/Hot-Tub-Filter-Cleaning-Made-Easy.html)). Sundance's guide lists a nearly identical set: permanent discoloration that doesn't improve with deep cleaning, crushed or matted pleats, a cracked end cap or core, persistent cloudy water despite correct chemistry, and frequent filter-related error messages on the control panel ([Sundance Spas](https://www.sundancespas.com/blog-articles/how-to-clean-a-hot-tub-filter-using-sundance-r-spa-filters-the-right-way)). Any of these point to replacement over another cleaning attempt, no matter how recently the filter was replaced.

## Documented age-based schedules vary considerably, sometimes within the same document

Jacuzzi's setup guide for new spas states, in its "Filter Installation and Maintenance" section, "Replace filter cartridges every 3-4 months under normal usage, or more frequently with heavy use" ([Jacuzzi](https://www.jacuzzi.com/en-us/Beginners-Guide-to-Setting-Up-Your-Jacuzzi.html)). That same figure is repeated later in the guide's "Quarterly Tasks" maintenance checklist, alongside a quarterly drain-and-refill, which suggests the 3-to-4-month figure may be tied to that quarterly water-change cycle rather than being an independent assessment of how long the filter media itself lasts. But the same guide also includes a separate "Annual Tasks" checklist that lists "Replace filter cartridges regardless of apparent condition" as a once-a-year task. Jacuzzi does not explain, in this document, how the quarterly and annual instructions are meant to work together. TubWise could not resolve this internal conflict from the source itself, so treat both figures as documented but unreconciled rather than assuming one supersedes the other.

A separate Jacuzzi filter-care guide gives a third figure: replacement every 12 to 24 months, or sooner if fibers are torn, flattened, or heavily stained ([Jacuzzi](https://www.jacuzzi.com/en-us/Hot-Tub-Filter-Cleaning-Made-Easy.html)). That guide states its recommendations apply to "typical pleated cartridge filters found in modern home hot tubs, including Jacuzzi models," which reads as general guidance for pleated cartridge filters rather than a Jacuzzi-specific engineering figure, even though it's published on Jacuzzi's own site.

Sundance Spas publishes a usage-based table: typical home use of 3 to 5 times weekly suggests replacement every 12 to 18 months, heavy or commercial use every 6 to 12 months, and light use of once weekly or less can stretch to 24 months with proper maintenance ([Sundance Spas](https://www.sundancespas.com/blog-articles/how-to-clean-a-hot-tub-filter-using-sundance-r-spa-filters-the-right-way)). This is the most internally consistent of the three sources reviewed, but it's specific to Sundance spas and pleated cartridges of the type Sundance uses.

Taken together, these sources describe replacement intervals ranging from 3 months to 2 years, including one internal conflict Jacuzzi's own document doesn't resolve. That range is too wide to collapse into a single number without guessing, which is exactly why your own owner's manual, and the physical signs of wear described above, matter more here than any age-based figure quoted in this article.

If a filter consistently needs replacing sooner than any of these intervals, even with regular cleaning, see [Why Does My Hot Tub Filter Get Dirty So Fast?](/maintenance/why-does-my-hot-tub-filter-get-dirty-so-fast) for documented reasons a filter can wear out faster than a general schedule assumes.

## What this means for your filter

Rather than relying on a single number, use the documented signs above as your primary trigger, and treat any age-based figure from your own manual as a secondary check. A filter that's cleaned on schedule and still shows no damage or performance issues may not need replacing on a fixed calendar date; one that shows the signs above may need replacing well before a general interval would suggest. See [How Often Should You Clean a Hot Tub Filter?](/maintenance/how-often-should-you-clean-a-hot-tub-filter) for the cleaning side of this routine.

Sundance's guidance offers a useful way to frame the decision: replacement becomes the right call once cleaning "saves you from expensive repairs and persistent water issues" that the filter itself can no longer prevent, rather than after any specific number of cleaning cycles ([Sundance Spas](https://www.sundancespas.com/blog-articles/how-to-clean-a-hot-tub-filter-using-sundance-r-spa-filters-the-right-way)). In practice, that means a filter that keeps failing to restore clear water or steady flow after a proper cleaning has likely reached the end of its useful life, even if it looks intact and even if it hasn't hit the interval printed in any manual.

## A note on cost and timing

Replacing a filter before it's strictly necessary is a low-risk decision; running a spa on a filter that's already showing the signs above is the higher-risk choice, since a filter that can no longer trap fine particles effectively puts more strain on the pump and heater over time. If you're between cleaning cycles and unsure whether a filter still has useful life left, it's reasonable to compare the cost of a new cartridge against the cost of a service call for a pump or heater issue that a failing filter may be contributing to.

## Safe checks the homeowner can do

1. **Check your own owner's manual first** for a model-specific replacement interval.
2. **Inspect the filter each time you clean it** for cracks, tears, collapsed pleats, or permanent discoloration. See [How to Clean a Hot Tub Filter](/maintenance/how-to-clean-a-hot-tub-filter) for the method itself.
3. **Track whether cleaning actually restores clarity and flow.** If it doesn't, that's a stronger signal than the filter's age.
4. **Watch for repeat flow or error codes** immediately after reinstalling a freshly cleaned filter.
5. **Keep a simple log** of replacement dates so you have a record to compare against your manual's guidance.
6. **Order a replacement before the old filter is unusable**, rather than running the spa without one while you wait. See [Can You Run a Hot Tub Without a Filter?](/maintenance/can-you-run-a-hot-tub-without-a-filter) for what manufacturers recommend in the meantime, and [How to Choose a Replacement Hot Tub Filter](/buying-guides/how-to-choose-a-replacement-hot-tub-filter) for part-number and fit checks before you buy.

## When to stop and call a technician

- Water stays cloudy or jets stay weak after installing a new filter and confirming water chemistry. See [Can a Dirty Filter Make Hot Tub Water Cloudy?](/water-care/can-a-dirty-filter-make-hot-tub-water-cloudy) and [Why Are My Hot Tub Jets Weak?](/troubleshooting/why-are-my-hot-tub-jets-weak)
- A flow or filter-related error code returns right after a new filter is installed
- You're not sure the replacement filter matches your spa's specifications

If a new filter doesn't resolve the problem, the issue likely isn't the filter itself, and qualified service is the more reliable next step.
