Yes. A dirty or clogged filter is listed by both Jacuzzi and Hot Spring as a cause of cloudy water, separate from chemistry problems. When a filter is clogged, water flow drops and small particles that would normally be trapped stay suspended, which shows up as haze.

## How a dirty filter causes cloudiness

Jacuzzi's cloudy-water guidance identifies dirty and clogged filters as a common cause, explaining that a clogged filter "reduces water flow and the effectiveness of sanitizers" ([Jacuzzi](https://www.jacuzzi.com/en-us/Troubleshooting-Cloudy-Hot-Tub-Water.html)). Hot Spring's own troubleshooting chart lists "dirty filters" as one of several probable causes of cloudy water, alongside chemistry issues ([Hot Spring](https://www.hotspring.com/owners/water-care-and-troubleshooting)). In both cases, the filter is treated as its own distinct cause — not just a side effect of bad chemistry.

### Filtration and chemistry can overlap

Jacuzzi's setup guide describes a troubleshooting sequence for cloudy water during new-spa startup that checks filtration first: confirm the filter cartridges are properly installed and clean, run the jets on high speed for about 30 minutes to help circulate and filter the water, and then retest chemistry ([Jacuzzi](https://www.jacuzzi.com/en-us/Beginners-Guide-to-Setting-Up-Your-Jacuzzi.html)). That sequence reflects that a dirty filter and poor water chemistry can produce similar-looking cloudy water, so it's worth checking both rather than assuming it's one or the other. Jacuzzi's installation guidance emphasizes proper filter seating to prevent bypass ([Jacuzzi](https://www.jacuzzi.com/en-us/Beginners-Guide-to-Setting-Up-Your-Jacuzzi.html)). If water is not moving through the filter media as intended, filtration may be less effective — though Jacuzzi addresses this as an installation step rather than as a documented cause of cloudiness specifically.

### A dirty filter can affect more than water clarity

If your spa's water is also slow to heat or the jets feel weak, a clogged filter can be a shared cause. See [Can a Dirty Filter Stop a Hot Tub From Heating?](/troubleshooting/can-a-dirty-filter-stop-a-hot-tub-from-heating) if you're noticing more than one symptom at once.

## How often filters should be cleaned

Manufacturer guidance varies by brand and filter type, so check your own owner's manual first. As documented examples:

- Jacuzzi's troubleshooting guidance recommends rinsing or backwashing filters every 4 to 6 weeks, and soaking filter elements in a filter-cleaning product at least once a month ([Jacuzzi](https://www.jacuzzi.com/en-us/Troubleshooting-Cloudy-Hot-Tub-Water.html)).
- Jacuzzi's setup guide separately recommends replacing filter cartridges every 3–4 months under normal use, more often with heavy use ([Jacuzzi](https://www.jacuzzi.com/en-us/Beginners-Guide-to-Setting-Up-Your-Jacuzzi.html)).

These figures come from Jacuzzi's own materials and may not apply to every brand or filter type. Filter media, cartridge size, and recommended cleaning products vary by manufacturer and by model, so follow the schedule and products listed in your own manual if it differs from the examples above.

## Safe checks the homeowner can do

1. **Remove the filter and inspect it.** Look for visible buildup, discoloration, or debris caught in the pleats.
2. **Confirm it's seated correctly.** Follow your owner's manual for the correct orientation — a filter that isn't properly installed can let unfiltered water bypass it.
3. **Rinse it with a garden hose** to clear surface debris, following your owner's manual for removal and reinstallation.
4. **Soak it in a filter-cleaning product** if it hasn't had a deep clean recently, following the product label's instructions.
5. **Run the jets** after cleaning to help circulate filtered water, then retest chemistry before deciding whether more correction is needed. See [How Long Does It Take Cloudy Hot Tub Water to Clear?](/water-care/how-long-does-it-take-cloudy-hot-tub-water-to-clear) if you're unsure how long to wait before rechecking.
6. **Check the filter's age.** If you can't remember the last time it was replaced, or it's past the interval in your manual, replacement may be the more reliable fix than repeated cleaning.
7. **Turn off power before handling the filter if your owner's manual instructs you to do so.**

## When to stop and call a technician

A dirty filter is a homeowner-serviceable issue, but stop and call a technician if:

- The filter looks fine and chemistry tests correctly, yet the water stays cloudy
- Water flow feels weak or absent even with a clean filter installed
- You notice damage to the filter housing, seals, or the area around the filter well

If cleaning and replacing the filter doesn't resolve the cloudiness, the filter isn't the cause, and it's worth looking at the water itself instead. See [Why Is My Hot Tub Water Cloudy?](/water-care/why-is-my-hot-tub-water-cloudy) for the fuller list of causes, or [Should You Drain a Hot Tub if the Water Is Cloudy?](/water-care/should-you-drain-a-hot-tub-if-the-water-is-cloudy) if the water may simply be old. Keeping a simple log of when you clean or replace the filter can also make it easier to rule filtration in or out the next time cloudiness comes up.
