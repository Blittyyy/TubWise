Brown hot tub water is not the same problem as green algae water. Hot Spring's Spa Chemistry 101 water-quality table lists **Brown Water** with high iron or manganese content and points to a product that decreases minerals in the water ([Hot Spring Spa Chemistry 101](https://www.hotspring.com/wp-content/uploads/2018/11/Spa-Chemistry-101.pdf)). Related Hot Spring manuals also link high iron or copper to green or brown stains on the spa ([Hot Spring Highlife Manual](https://www.hotspring.com/wp-content/uploads/2023/07/highlife-owners-manual-en_us.pdf)).

If the color showed up during or right after a refill, also see [Why Is My Hot Tub Water Discolored After Filling?](/water-care/why-is-my-hot-tub-water-discolored-after-filling). If the water is green rather than brown, see [Why Is My Hot Tub Water Green?](/water-care/why-is-my-hot-tub-water-green).

## Safety note

Color alone does not prove the spa is safe to use. Test sanitizer and pH before soaking. Follow your spa manual and chemical product labels. Do not mix different pool or spa chemicals. If you treat for metals with FreshWater Stain & Scale in a Hot Spring water-care context, Hot Spring's metals FAQ directs owners to wait 24 hours before adding chlorine or other oxidizers ([Hot Spring](https://www.hotspring.com/faq/how-do-i-reduce-metals-my-water)). Confirm amounts and wait times on the product label and your manual.

## Causes to check

### High iron or manganese in the water

Hot Spring's Spa Chemistry 101 table names high iron or manganese content for brown water ([Hot Spring Spa Chemistry 101](https://www.hotspring.com/wp-content/uploads/2018/11/Spa-Chemistry-101.pdf)). Hot Spring's metals FAQ likewise names iron or copper as examples of metals that can complicate spa water care depending on location or well water ([Hot Spring](https://www.hotspring.com/faq/how-do-i-reduce-metals-my-water)). The Highlife Collection owner's manual recommends a Clean Screen pre-filter at fill and top-off for iron, copper, and manganese ([Hot Spring Highlife Manual](https://www.hotspring.com/wp-content/uploads/2023/07/highlife-owners-manual-en_us.pdf)).

### Metals reacting after sanitizer is added

Hot Spring's spa-chemicals FAQ states that metals such as iron and manganese can interact with chlorine and discolor spa water ([Hot Spring](https://www.hotspring.com/faq/how-do-i-choose-the-right-spa-chemicals-for-my-hot-tub)). If the water looked clearer during the fill and then turned brown after chlorine or another oxidizer went in, that timing matches this manufacturer description. It is still not proof of which metal is present.

### Low pH or alkalinity plus metals

Hot Spring's troubleshooting chart lists stains with total alkalinity and/or pH too low, and high iron or copper in source water ([Hot Spring](https://www.hotspring.com/owners/water-care-and-troubleshooting)). Aggressive low-pH water and metals can both matter. Do not treat brown color as proof that chemistry is already balanced.

### What this is not

- **Green algae water** has a different manufacturer cause list (high pH and low sanitizer on Jacuzzi and Hot Spring algae rows). See [Why Is My Hot Tub Water Green?](/water-care/why-is-my-hot-tub-water-green).
- **Hazy cloudy water** without a brown tint has its own cause list. See [Why Is My Hot Tub Water Cloudy?](/water-care/why-is-my-hot-tub-water-cloudy).
- **Rust-colored stains on the shell** are a surface problem related to metals, not the same as brown bulk water. See [Why Does My Hot Tub Have Rust-Colored Stains?](/water-care/why-does-my-hot-tub-have-rust-colored-stains).

## Safe checks you can do

1. **Note when the brown color appeared.** During fill, right after sanitizer, or days later.
2. **Test pH, alkalinity, and sanitizer first.** Confirm whether chemistry is in your manual's range before adding more products. See [How Often Should You Test Hot Tub Water?](/maintenance/how-often-should-you-test-hot-tub-water).
3. **Ask about the fill source and pre-filter.** Well water and mineral-heavy municipal water are the contexts Hot Spring names for metals ([Hot Spring](https://www.hotspring.com/faq/how-do-i-reduce-metals-my-water); [Hot Spring Highlife Manual](https://www.hotspring.com/wp-content/uploads/2023/07/highlife-owners-manual-en_us.pdf)).
4. **If metals are confirmed or strongly suspected, follow metal-treatment sequencing for your brand.** Hot Spring's metals FAQ points to filling with Clean Screen, then using FreshWater Stain & Scale, then waiting 24 hours before chlorine or other oxidizers ([Hot Spring](https://www.hotspring.com/faq/how-do-i-reduce-metals-my-water)). Use the amounts on your product label.
5. **Do not fill from swimming-pool water.** Hot Spring's Highlife fill instructions say pool chemicals differ from spa chemicals and can damage the spa ([Hot Spring Highlife Manual](https://www.hotspring.com/wp-content/uploads/2023/07/highlife-owners-manual-en_us.pdf)).
6. **Retest after any treatment wait time** before soaking.

## When to stop and get help

Stop adding oxidizers and stop guessing from color if:

- Brown color continues after a labeled metal treatment and the wait time your brand requires
- You cannot confirm sanitizer and pH in range
- Staining on the shell returns after cleaning ([Why Does My Hot Tub Have Rust-Colored Stains?](/water-care/why-does-my-hot-tub-have-rust-colored-stains))
- Cloudiness remains after chemistry and filtration checks

Contact your dealer or a qualified spa technician, especially for well water with unknown metal content. For drain timing, see [How Often Should You Drain and Refill a Hot Tub?](/maintenance/how-often-should-you-drain-and-refill-a-hot-tub).
