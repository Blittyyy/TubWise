## Direct answer

CDC home pool and hot tub guidance says to test chlorine concentration and pH at least twice per day, and more often when many people are using the spa (CDC). Manufacturer maintenance pages can list a different cadence for routine ownership: Hot Spring's maintenance schedule includes weekly testing of pH, alkalinity, hardness, and sanitizer (Hot Spring), and Jacuzzi's beginner guide tells owners to test chemical levels twice weekly for ongoing maintenance (Jacuzzi). Those sources are answering related but not identical questions. Use CDC for sanitizer and pH safety checks, and use your spa manual for the full parameter list and brand-specific balance targets.

## Safety note

Follow the directions that come with your test kit and chemical products. Do not mix chlorine and bromine systems. Do not enter the spa when sanitizer or pH is out of the range your manual and public-health guidance require. One test result does not prove the water is safe for every health condition. Adjust chemicals only with labeled spa products and confirmed water volume.

## Why published schedules do not all match

CDC focuses on free chlorine (or bromine elsewhere in CDC hot-tub materials) and pH as the first defense against germs that can cause swimming-related illness, and it tells residential owners to check those values at least twice a day (CDC). Hot Spring and Jacuzzi publish ownership checklists that fold testing into weekly or twice-weekly water-care routines alongside alkalinity, hardness, and other parameters (Hot Spring; Jacuzzi).

Do not flatten those into one invented schedule. If your manual asks for weekly strip testing of a full panel, that does not erase CDC's twice-daily chlorine and pH check. If you test chlorine and pH twice daily, you still need the broader balance checks your brand documents.

## What to test

### Sanitizer and pH first

CDC recommends at least 3 ppm free chlorine in hot tubs and pH 7.0 to 7.8 (CDC). Separate CDC hot-tub guidance lists bromine at 4 to 8 ppm with the same pH band (CDC). CDC also recommends not using cyanuric acid or chlorine products that contain cyanuric acid in hot tubs (CDC).

### Other parameters on manufacturer schedules

Hot Spring's weekly list includes alkalinity and hardness along with sanitizer and pH (Hot Spring). Jacuzzi's setup and maintenance guidance tracks alkalinity, pH, calcium hardness, and sanitizer in sequence for balancing, and tells owners to enter only when chemistry is properly balanced (Jacuzzi). Exact numeric targets belong in your manual and product labels, not in a universal TubWise table.

For choosing strips versus a liquid DPD kit, see [What to Look for in a Hot Tub Water Test Kit](/buying-guides/what-to-look-for-in-a-hot-tub-water-test-kit). CDC states a DPD test kit gives more accurate chlorine and pH results than test strips, though strips can still be used (CDC).

## Practical testing cadence for homeowners

1. **Before soaking, and again later the same day when the spa sees use:** check free chlorine or bromine and pH against CDC's hot-tub ranges (CDC).
2. **On your brand's weekly or twice-weekly day:** run the fuller panel your manual lists (Hot Spring; Jacuzzi).
3. **After heavy bather load or visible water problems:** retest sanitizer and pH sooner (CDC).
4. **After adding chemicals:** wait the circulation time your label and manual require before deciding the adjustment worked (Jacuzzi notes waiting between major adjustments in its setup sequence).

Place testing on your broader calendar with [Hot Tub Maintenance Checklist: Weekly, Monthly, and Seasonal Tasks](/maintenance/hot-tub-maintenance-checklist-weekly-monthly-and-seasonal-tasks).

## What testing does not replace

Testing does not replace filter cleaning, scheduled drains, or dealing with foam, cloudiness, or odor. Low sanitizer with foam or cloudiness is a stay-out signal until chemistry is corrected; see [Is Foamy Hot Tub Water Safe to Use?](/water-care/is-foamy-hot-tub-water-safe-to-use) and [Why Is My Hot Tub Water Cloudy?](/water-care/why-is-my-hot-tub-water-cloudy).

## When to stop and call a technician or dealer

- Readings stay out of range after label-supported adjustments
- Test kits expire, bleach out above about 10 ppm chlorine, or give conflicting results you cannot resolve (CDC warns high chlorine can bleach DPD tests)
- Your spa uses a salt or automatic monitoring system that shows faults your manual routes to the dealer
- You need help matching a sanitizer system; see [Chlorine vs. Bromine for Hot Tubs](/buying-guides/chlorine-vs-bromine-for-hot-tubs)
