## Direct answer

A bad smell from a hot tub cover can be surface mildew or grime on the vinyl, or, when Hot Spring connects the cues, a musty odor that may point to mold growing inside the foam core (Hot Spring). Clean the cover with the mild methods your manufacturer documents before you assume the foam is ruined. A cover odor is also not the same problem as spa water that smells while you soak; treat those as separate checks.

## Safety note

Remove the cover before using soap so residue does not enter the water (Hot Spring Highlife owner's manual; Jacuzzi). Use only cleaners your spa or cover maker allows. Jacuzzi warns against household products and steel wool on vinyl covers (Jacuzzi). Do not walk or sit on the cover (Hot Spring Highlife owner's manual). Do not mix bleach, solvents, or pressure washing into the job unless your exact cover manual allows that product and method.

## Separate the smell sources

### Surface mildew or grime on the cover

Hot Spring states that mold and mildew frequently first appear on a hot tub cover and can then spread to the water (Hot Spring). The same Hot Spring article says surface grime or mildew on a cover can transfer into the spa water (Hot Spring, replacement-care guidance). Jacuzzi's swim-spa page notes that wiping the underside in humid climates can reduce mildew buildup that may transfer to spa water and cause odors (Jacuzzi).

For cleaning steps, follow [How to Clean a Hot Tub Cover](/maintenance/how-to-clean-a-hot-tub-cover). Hot Spring's mold article directs owners to remove a moldy cover, spray with a dish-soap and water mixture, wipe it down, then condition with Cover Shield (Hot Spring). Highlife manuals document monthly vinyl cleaning with a mild dishwashing solution or baking soda on the top and water only on the underside (Hot Spring Highlife owner's manual).

### Musty smell that may involve the foam

Hot Spring's replacement article asks whether the cover smells like must or mold and says a bad smell **might** indicate mold growing inside the foam core (Hot Spring). That is a possibility Hot Spring lists among age and overuse signs, not a lab confirmation from odor alone.

Hot Spring separately defines a waterlogged cover as moisture through a failed vapor barrier into the foam, creating an environment for bacteria and mildew (Hot Spring). Hot Spring's mildew article adds that a cover grown increasingly heavier with water saturation may need replacement because a waterlogged foam core can breed mold (Hot Spring). Connect odor to foam-core moisture only when those manufacturer cues line up (musty smell plus rising weight or visible moisture damage), and see [Why Is My Hot Tub Cover So Heavy?](/maintenance/why-is-my-hot-tub-cover-so-heavy).

Jacuzzi's swim-spa cover page groups moldy smells, visible mildew, or dark underside spots as moisture problems that will not resolve without replacement on that page (Jacuzzi). Scope that replacement framing to Jacuzzi's swim-spa guidance when you use it.

### Spa water odor while soaking

If the smell is strongest in the water during a soak, start with water-care checks in [Why Does My Hot Tub Water Smell Bad?](/water-care/why-does-my-hot-tub-water-smell-bad) rather than assuming the cover foam is the only cause. Hot Spring's mildew article treats musty or moldy odor while soaking as a spa contamination path that needs water testing and sanitation work (Hot Spring).

## Safe checks

1. **Smell the top and the underside separately** after you remove the cover.
2. **Look for visible mildew patches** on vinyl and underside fabric.
3. **Clean with manufacturer-allowed mild soap methods**, then let the cover air.
4. **Recheck weight** after the exterior is dry. Rising weight plus musty smell fits Hot Spring's waterlogged and foam-mold language more closely (Hot Spring).
5. **Leave the cover off about half an hour after adding water-care chemicals** so vapors are not trapped against the cover (Hot Spring).
6. **Put cover inspection on your annual list** for mildew, moisture, and damage (Hot Spring).

## What not to do

- Do not soak the cover in bleach, ammonia, or solvent cocktails unless your exact manual allows that chemistry.
- Do not unzip and bleach foam cores from generic internet steps that your cover maker does not publish.
- Do not treat a cleaned surface smell that returns immediately the same way as a one-time dirty-cover smell; Hot Spring and Jacuzzi both point persistent foam-core moisture problems toward replacement (Hot Spring; Jacuzzi).
- Do not skip water testing if bathers notice odor in the spa itself.

## When to replace or call a dealer

If a proper cleaning does not clear a musty cover smell, or if the cover is also much heavier or waterlogged by Hot Spring's definition, plan replacement (Hot Spring; Jacuzzi).
